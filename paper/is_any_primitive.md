# Is Any of Them Primitive? The Symmetric Circularity of Consent, Non-Domination, Capability, and Rights

*The fair attack. [`consent_not_foundational.md`](consent_not_foundational.md) showed
FDK's primitive — consent — is not foundational: it presupposes autonomy, competence,
domination, capability. Honesty forbids stopping there. The same scalpel must be turned
on the **rivals'** primitives, above all on Pettit's non-domination, which has so far
been used to convict FDK without being put on trial itself. If non-domination (and
capability, and self-ownership) are **also** non-primitive, the verdict does not become
"FDK wins" — FDK is still a reducible projection — but the dialectic changes shape: the
question stops being "which theory is true?" and becomes "is any of these concepts the
foundation, or are they a web with no floor?"*

## 0. Why this is not special pleading

A tempting bad move: "consent isn't primitive, but non-domination isn't either, so FDK is
fine after all." That inference is invalid and this paper does not make it. FDK's lack of
a distinctive verdict-column ([`reverse_test.md`](reverse_test.md)) and its failure to
survive Pettit as a rival ([`steelman_pettit.md`](steelman_pettit.md)) stand regardless of
whether Pettit is foundational. What changes if the rivals are *also* non-primitive is
narrower and still important: the objection "consent is derivative" loses its force *as a
unique indictment of FDK*, because being derivative turns out to be the universal
condition of political-philosophical primitives — and the real frontier is exposed.

## 1. Non-domination on trial (the same four sub-questions)

Pettit: freedom is **non-domination** — not being subject to another's *capacity* for
**arbitrary** interference. Apply Agent A's test: can "domination" be specified without
borrowing?

**1a. Domination without RIGHTS / a baseline?** The load-bearing word is *arbitrary*.
Pettit: interference is arbitrary when not forced to track the avowable interests and
ideas of the person subject to it; non-arbitrary interference (a just, contestable law
that jails a thief) is *not* domination. But this requires, prior to the analysis, (i) a
line between **interference** and mere *affecting* (my competing with you in a market
affects you but does not interfere — and that line is the same moralised baseline that
fixes coercion), and (ii) a standard of which interferences are *legitimate*
(non-arbitrary). Both are a prior theory of **rights/legitimate power**. The long critique
of Pettit on exactly this point — is "arbitrary" *moralised* (then it presupposes a prior
account of legitimate interests, i.e. rights) or *procedural* (then it cannot tell good
interference from bad)? (Kramer; Carter; Larmore; Arneson) — is the non-domination twin of
the consent-circularity. **Rights are upstream of domination.**

**1b. Domination without AGENCY / AUTONOMY?** Only a being with *avowable interests and
ideas* can be dominated; a landslide is not a tyrant. "Power that fails to track your
interests" presupposes a subject who *has* interests and a capacity to *avow* them — i.e.
an autonomous agent. The dominable subject is the autonomous subject, exactly as the
consenting subject was. **Agency is upstream of domination.**

**1c. Domination without CAPABILITY?** Pettit's remedies — effective **contestation**,
secured **exit**, the "eyeball test" (you can look others in the eye without reason for
fear) — are not satisfied by formal permission; they require the *real powers* to
contest and to leave. That is a threshold of **capability** (Sen). Non-domination and
capability interpenetrate: a secured exit you lack the resources to take is no exit, and
saying so is Sen's point, not a new one. **Capability is upstream of (effective)
non-domination.**

**1d. Domination without a CONSENT-like notion?** "Avowable" interests — those you *would*
endorse under suitable conditions — is a **counterfactual-consent** idiom. The
non-arbitrary tracks what you could be brought to accept; the dominator imposes what you
could not. Non-domination thus leans on a hypothetical-endorsement notion that is consent's
own family. The two primitives lean on **each other**.

**Verdict on 1.** Non-domination is **not primitive**. It presupposes rights (to fix
arbitrary/interference), agency (to have a dominable, interest-bearing subject),
capability (for effective contestation/exit), and a counterfactual-consent notion (avowal).
It is a node, by exactly the argument that unseated consent.

## 2. Capability and self-ownership, briefly (the same fate)

**Capability (Sen) is not primitive.** A capability is the real freedom to achieve
functionings *one has reason to value*. "Has reason to value" imports a theory of
**practical reason and the good** (which functionings count? — Nussbaum answers with an
openly substantive list); and "whose capability?" imports **agency**. Capability
presupposes a value-theory plus agency. Node, not root.

**Self-ownership / rights (Nozick) is not primitive.** "You own yourself" presupposes (i)
a **self/agent** to be owned (agency again), (ii) an analysis of *owning* as a bundle of
Hohfeldian incidents — control, use, income, transfer, exclusion — which are *prior
normative relations*, so ownership is defined in terms of rights rather than grounding
them, and (iii) an **acquisition rule** for original title that no consent act supplies
(the genesis regress). Node, not root.

## 3. The shape that emerges: a web, not a foundation

Lay the four side by side and the dependencies form a cycle, not a hierarchy:

| Primitive | Presupposes |
|---|---|
| **Consent** | autonomy, competence, rights/coercion-baseline, capability, property |
| **Non-domination** | rights/legitimacy-baseline, agency, capability, avowal (≈ consent) |
| **Capability** | a theory of the good/practical reason, agency |
| **Self-ownership / rights** | agency, prior Hohfeldian incidents, an acquisition rule |

No member is the floor. Each is specifiable only through some of the others. The
recurring substratum under all four is the pair **(agency/autonomy, a baseline of
legitimate interference)** — and *those* in turn are contested and arguably interdefined
(an agent's standing is partly constituted by her rights; her rights presuppose her
agency). **There is no Archimedean primitive among the going theories of political
freedom.** Contemporary political philosophy is a web of mutually presupposing concepts,
and the search for *the* foundation — consent, or liberty-as-non-domination, or
capability, or self-ownership — is, on this evidence, a search for a floor that is not
there.

## 4. What this does and does not do for FDK

- **Does not rescue FDK's distinctiveness.** FDK still exhibits no independent
  verdict-column; RC is still a projection of Nozick ∪ Pettit; consent is still
  derivative. The negative verdict of the other papers is untouched.
- **Does demote the indictment.** "Consent is not foundational" stops being *FDK's
  peculiar flaw* and becomes an *instance of a universal condition*. Pettit convicts FDK
  of non-foundationalism while standing on the same non-foundation. That is worth saying
  out loud, because it relocates the contest: the rivals are not *more foundational* than
  FDK; they are *better developed* and (Pettit) *strictly stronger on verdicts*. FDK's
  problem is not "you built on sand" — everyone did — but "you built less, and later, on
  the same sand."

## 5. What is genuinely, sharply alive (and it is no longer FDK's question)

Strip away the theory-branding and one question survives the whole demolition:

> **Is there a structural criterion — for domination, for valid consent, for dependence,
> for exit — that does NOT bottom out in agents' subjective preferences, and yet does NOT
> import a perfectionist theory of the good?**

Every theory faces the same fork and each takes a side: index to **subjective preference**
and the contented slave / adaptive preference defeats you (Rothbard; thin consent); index
to **objective interests / the good** and you are a paternalist or perfectionist (Sen's
capability list; Raz). The open frontier — the one neither preference-subjectivism nor
perfectionism occupies — is a **third thing: a relational/structural property of an
arrangement** that is mind-independent of the participant's current wants without being a
ranking of how she ought to live. Pettit's *secured exit / contestation* is one candidate
for such a structural marker. FDK's *preserved exit right* is another. **They are very
plausibly the same marker reached by two routes** — and that convergence, rather than
being only FDK's defeat, is weak positive evidence that *exit-structure* is the most
promising candidate for a preference-independent, non-perfectionist criterion of freedom.

That is the one constructive residue of the entire negative program: not that FDK is a new
paradigm (it is not), but that the place where FDK and Pettit collide — **the structural
exit/contestation marker** — is the live frontier of the *shared* problem, and is worth a
research life precisely because it belongs to all of them and is owned by none.

## 6. The honest status, as an academic referee would write it

- **Achievements:** turned a vague idea into a set of precise claims; documented its
  failure points instead of hiding them; made the critiques the center of the project;
  generated *real* questions — and, in this paper, generalised the critique to show the
  whole field shares FDK's non-foundationalism.
- **Failures:** FDK's theoretical distinctiveness is unproven; consent's primitiveness has
  collapsed; "protection without paternalism" is most likely already held by Pettit.
- **What is alive:** not FDK in its original form, but the structural-criterion question of
  §5 — now correctly seen as a question for Pettit, Sen, Nozick, and nearly all of
  contemporary political philosophy, not for FDK alone.

In serious research this is not a defeat. Mapping *exactly where a new paradigm is not* is
itself a contribution: it shrinks the space of possibilities and marks which roads are
dead ends. FDK's most defensible legacy may be that it walked several of those roads to
their ends carefully enough to report back that they are closed — and pointed, on the way
out, at the one junction that is still open.
