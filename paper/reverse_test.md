# The Reverse Vocabulary Test: Does FDK Produce an Independent Column?

*A correction to method. The 61-case bank started from FDK and sorted cases into
buckets named Rothbard / Sen / Pettit — so "zero fourth routes" partly just shows the
buckets were exhaustive **by construction**. A hostile referee's reply writes itself:
"You searched for a fourth route only inside a taxonomy already defined by your three
rivals." This paper removes that bias by reversing the search: start from the **rivals'
own** distinctive cases — derived from their commitments, not FDK's — confirm each
rival carves a genuinely independent column, and only then ask whether FDK produces a
column at all.*

Draft for hostile review.*

## 0. The epistemic correction, stated plainly

Two sentences are not equivalent, and the earlier files used the stronger one:

- ✗ "Zero of the 61 cases yielded a fourth route." (reads as: none exists)
- ✓ "No fourth route appeared **in the sampled cases, under a rubric whose categories
  were the three rivals**." (the actual epistemic content)

The case bank is **suggestive, not probative**, and adding 600 more cases under the
same rubric would not fix the bias — it would amplify it. The defect is not sample
size; it is that the search space was carved by the rivals. The remedy is a different
test, below. (The case-bank files are retained but should be read with this caveat;
they are now annotated to point here.)

## 1. The reverse method

For each rival, exhibit a case that **only that rival** gets right — a case the other
two cannot account for from their own commitments. If each rival has such a cell, the
three are genuinely independent columns (not collapsible into one another), and the
table has real structure. Then pose the same demand to FDK: **exhibit a case that only
FDK gets right.** If FDK can, it has an independent column. If it cannot — if every
verdict it gives is one a rival already gives, and it is *silent* exactly where it might
have differed — then FDK is a *projection* of the rivals, not a fourth column. Crucially,
this conclusion is reached without ever using FDK's taxonomy.

## 2. Each rival carves a real, independent cell

**Nozick's distinctive cell — the injustice of correction.** A person freely earns a
fortune in clean transactions (Wilt Chamberlain); the state taxes it to reduce
inequality. *Only Nozick* says the **redistribution itself is an injustice** — a
violation of an entitlement that arose from legitimate steps. Sen sees the tax as
serving capability; Pettit sees no domination in it (a non-arbitrary, contestable law);
neither can generate "correcting the pattern is *wrong*." This verdict is Nozick's
alone.

**Sen's distinctive cell — agentless capability loss.** A blight destroys a region's
crops; no one is at fault, no contract was broken, no person wields power over the
victims. They lose the capability to be nourished. *Only Sen* calls this a loss of
**freedom** demanding response. Nozick: no rights were violated, so no injustice.
Pettit: there is no dominating agent, so no loss of non-domination. The deprivation is
real and *agentless*, and only the capability vocabulary registers it as a freedom
question.

**Pettit's distinctive cell — the happy slave of a benevolent master.** A servant is
owned by a kind master who never interferes, provides amply (her welfare and
capabilities are high), and she is content and does not wish to leave. *Only Pettit*
says she is **unfree** — because the master *retains the arbitrary power* to interfere
whether or not he uses it; domination is modal. Nozick: she consented / no rights are
being violated → permissible. Sen: her capabilities are well met → no capability
deficit. Only the non-domination vocabulary condemns un-exercised, contented mastery.

These three cells are mutually irreducible: each is a case the other two vocabularies
cannot reach. So the rivals are three real columns, and the table is not degenerate.
Good — that is what makes the next question meaningful.

## 3. Now FDK: exhibit a case only the exit-right condition gets right

FDK's signature is RC: *valid consent requires a preserved exit right.* For FDK to have
an independent column, there must be a case where Nozick, Sen, and Pettit all give the
**wrong or no** verdict and RC gives the **right** one. Walk the candidates — the only
places RC could differ are about exit — and they exhaust into the rivals:

- **Exit foreclosed by an agent** (the master locks the door; the firm confiscates the
  passport; the platform forbids data export). RC: illegitimate (exit removed). But a
  foreclosed exit imposed by an agent *is* subjection to that agent's arbitrary power —
  **Pettit's domination**. RC's verdict here is Pettit's, reached by a narrower route.
  **Column = Pettit.**
- **Exit retained but unwanted** (the contented slave who could leave; the willing
  addict; the content cult member). RC: legitimate (exit preserved) — which is
  **Nozick's** "she consented." And it is the verdict Pettit calls *wrong* (she is still
  dominated). So here RC not only coincides with Nozick, it **fails Pettit's
  distinctive cell** (§2). **Column = Nozick (and wrong by Pettit's lights).**
- **Exit foreclosed by impersonal circumstance** (trapped by geography, market
  structure, or disability — no agent removed the exit). This is the *one* place RC
  might have carved something new. But RC, like FDK's whole gate, needs an **actor**
  whose boundary-crossing it can deny; an agentless foreclosure has no actor, so **RC is
  silent** (no verdict). And who speaks here? **Sen** — agentless capability loss is
  precisely his cell (§2). So in the single location where FDK might have been distinct,
  it is *mute*, and the case belongs to Sen.

There is no fourth position. RC's verdicts are the **projection** of Nozick (retained
exit → permit) ∪ Pettit (agent-foreclosed exit → forbid), and RC is **silent exactly
where it might have differed** (impersonal foreclosure), a silence Sen fills. FDK does
not produce an independent column; it produces a *shadow* cast by two of the three
existing columns, with a hole in it.

## 4. What this shows that the case bank could not

The reverse test answers the taxonomy-bias objection head-on, because the three test
cases in §2 were generated from **the rivals' commitments**, not from FDK's buckets. The
demand on FDK in §3 was the symmetric one each rival passed — *show the case only you get
right* — and FDK failed it: every verdict it issues is a rival's, and at its one
candidate-distinctive case it is silent. This is a stronger negative result than "0/61,"
and it is not an artifact of how the search space was carved.

It remains, in strict logic, **"no independent column has been exhibited,"** not "none
can exist" — exhibiting one would refute this paper in a sentence. But the burden is now
fully and correctly placed: the rivals each *showed* their signature case; FDK has been
asked for its signature case and produced none. A theory that cannot name a single
verdict that is distinctively its own has, on the table of verdicts, no claim to be a
distinct theory. (Its claim, if any, must be foundational — and that is exactly the
claim [`consent_not_foundational.md`](consent_not_foundational.md) argues also fails.)

## 5. The honest challenge to the author (the way to overturn this)

This paper is refuted by **one** case meeting all of:

1. an intuitively clear verdict that
2. Nozick's entitlement view gets wrong or cannot reach, **and**
3. Sen's capability view gets wrong or cannot reach, **and**
4. Pettit's non-domination view gets wrong or cannot reach, **and**
5. FDK's exit-right condition gets **right**, **without** importing welfare, capability,
   autonomy, or domination to do so.

If that case exists, FDK has a column and the project has, for the first time, a real
philosophical claim. The discipline of the last several files is that none of us —
across a forward case bank, five enemy dossiers, and now a reverse test — has produced
it. Finding it is the *entire* remaining philosophical task; until it is found, the
honest description stands: **FDK is a precise, deployable formalisation whose verdicts
are a projection of existing theories, strongest where it merely operationalises
Pettit, silent where Sen alone speaks.**

## 6. What not to do next

- **Not** another case bank — the bias is in the method, not the count.
- **Not** more engineering — no proof bears on conceptual priority.
- **The only moves that can change the verdict:** (a) exhibit the §5 case, or (b) close
  the consent-internal derivation of RC without an imported premise (three_questions Q2),
  or (c) refute the circularity argument (consent_not_foundational). All three are
  armchair philosophy against named opponents (Pettit, Sen, Raz, Owens), not
  development tasks. That is where the project now lives — or doesn't.
