# FDK — the critical papers (read these before the code)

> If you are a hostile referee deciding whether FDK is a philosophical contribution,
> do not start with the README, the tests, or the Lean proofs. Those establish only
> that the system is *consistent given its axiom* — they are silent on whether the
> axiom is *true* or *new*. Start here. These papers were written to **destroy** the
> central hypothesis, not defend it, and they mostly succeed. That is the result.

## The hypothesis on trial

> **(RC) Valid consent requires a preserved exit right** — FDK's one candidate for a
> distinctive, correct contribution. Everything else in FDK is redundant with
> libertarianism or already refuted (Parfit on standing, Ostrom on the commons). So
> RC's fate is FDK's fate *as philosophy*.

## The convergent verdict

Five independent attacks, by different routes, reach the same place:

1. **RC is not new** — its protective content is in Mill (inalienable liberty),
   Hirschman (exit), Wertheimer (coerced baseline), and above all **Pettit**
   (non-domination), which is strictly stronger on the decisive contented cases.
2. **RC is not self-grounding** — "consent without exit is invalid" cannot be derived
   from consent without importing self-ownership, autonomy, or non-domination (Q2).
3. **FDK has no verdict-column of its own** — every verdict it issues is a rival's, and
   at its one candidate-distinctive case (impersonal foreclosure) it is *silent*.
4. **FDK does not survive Pettit** as a rival theory — only as a machine-checkable
   *operationalisation* of the exit-dimension of non-domination.
5. **Consent is not even the right foundation** — it is downstream of the very concepts
   (autonomy, competence, domination, capability, property) FDK hoped to build out of it.

**Therefore:** FDK is, on present evidence, **not a new philosophical paradigm and not
yet a distinct moral theory.** It is best and honestly described as a *precise,
machine-checked, deployable operationalisation of an existing family of ideas* — a
consent-**checker** that presupposes a rights/agency/property baseline it does not
supply, whose verdicts project onto Nozick and Pettit. **This is not a failure.** A
clean, executable authorisation gate for AI agents has real engineering and product
value regardless of philosophical novelty; many influential artifacts are exactly this.
The discipline these papers enforce is only that the two claims — *useful formalisation*
and *new paradigm* — must never be confused.

## What is actually left (and it is small, precise, and not code)

One thread is uncut, and it is **foundational, not rival**:

> Can the inalienability of the exit right be grounded in the structure of **normative
> powers** without importing a person-premise — and if it cannot, is the premise it
> needs better supplied by self-ownership, autonomy, or non-domination?

That is a problem in the philosophy of normative powers (Raz; Owens, *Shaping the
Normative Landscape*; Hohfeld; Radin, *Market-Inalienability*; Kant's *Doctrine of
Right*), to be settled by argument against named opponents — most likely by *confirming*
the circularity, which is itself a publishable negative result. No proof, benchmark, or
layer can move it.

The single empirical question that would overturn the whole negative verdict is the
**reverse test's open challenge** (paper 3, §5): exhibit *one* case with a clear verdict
that Nozick, Sen, *and* Pettit each get wrong or cannot reach, and that RC gets right
*without* importing welfare, capability, autonomy, or domination. None of these papers —
nor a forward case bank, five dossiers, and a reverse test — has produced it. Finding it
is the entire remaining philosophical task.
