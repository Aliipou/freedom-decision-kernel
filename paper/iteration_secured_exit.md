# Iterating the Survivor: Is "Secured Exit" the Structural Criterion — or Only the Instrument?

*"So it's bullshit?" No — and the iteration below says exactly why, by pushing the one
surviving thread until it either becomes something real or dies cleanly. The grand claim
(a new paradigm) is dead. This paper asks whether the modest claim has a precise,
defensible core, and finds one — smaller and sharper than hoped, but not nothing.*

*Theory: نظریه آزادی (Theory of Freedom), Mohammad Ali Jannat Khah Doust (CC BY 4.0).

## 0. What "bullshit" would have been (and why this isn't it)

Bullshit, in Frankfurt's sense, is speech indifferent to truth — here, it would have been
continuing to call FDK a "new paradigm" while the holes were known. The honest output of
the prior papers is the opposite: a documented, convergent **negative** result —
RC is reducible (three_questions), produces no independent column (reverse_test), does not
survive Pettit as a rival (steelman_pettit), and rests on a non-primitive concept that the
rivals share (consent_not_foundational, is_any_primitive). A negative result reached by
honest attack is a contribution; it tells you which roads are closed. So: not bullshit —
but the burden is to show the *residue* is real, not a face-saving consolation. That is
this paper's job.

## 1. The survivor, restated

One thread outlived the demolition (is_any_primitive §5): exit-structure as a candidate
for the empty third horn — **a criterion of freedom that is neither preference-subjective
(the contented slave defeats it) nor perfectionist (it does not rank how she should
live).** Pettit's *secured exit/contestation* and FDK's *preserved exit right* converge on
it from two routes. The iteration: sharpen FDK's version until it either claims the horn or
is shown to be a sub-case.

## 2. Iteration 1 — make the exit right MODAL, not occurrent

FDK's exit right, as written, is **occurrent**: is an exit *available now*, taken or not?
That is exactly why it failed the contented slave (exit available, unwanted → RC permits).
Replace it with a **modal** condition:

> **(RC*) Secured exit.** An ongoing authority relation is legitimate only if the
> subordinate party's exit **cannot be unilaterally foreclosed by the dominant party.**
> What matters is not whether she leaves, nor whether she currently *can*, but whether the
> counterparty holds the **power to shut the door** at will.

Re-run the contented slave. The door is open and she does not want to leave — but the
master **retains the power to lock it tomorrow**. Under RC* her exit is *not secured*, so
the relation is illegitimate — **and this verdict is reached without judging her
preference and without ranking her life.** It is a fact about the *master's powers*, not
about her wants. RC* therefore clears node K, where occurrent-RC could not: it is
preference-independent and non-perfectionist. This is real progress, and it is the first
time anything in the program has touched the contented slave without becoming Sen.

## 3. Iteration 1's cost — RC* is Pettit's exit-dimension, exactly

The progress comes at the predictable price. RC*'s move — *illegitimacy lives in the
counterparty's retained power, not in interference or in preference* — **is** Pettit's
modal account of domination, restricted to one dimension (the power over exit). So:

- RC* ⊂ non-domination, now provably and by construction: a *secured exit* is the
  non-existence of one arbitrary power (the power to foreclose exit), and non-domination is
  the non-existence of arbitrary power *überhaupt*.
- RC* is therefore **strictly narrower**: it is silent on a relation where exit is secured
  but the dominant party retains *other* arbitrary powers (to surveil, to punish within the
  relation, to alter terms). Pettit condemns that; RC* does not.

So iteration 1 confirms the reduction it was trying to escape: the only way to fix RC for
the contented slave is to make it modal, and making it modal makes it a *named fragment of
Pettit*. The conceptual horn is Pettit's, not FDK's. **As a theory, FDK still loses.**

## 4. Iteration 2 — does "secured exit" itself reduce? (Yes, as a norm; no, as a fact)

Push once more, on RC* itself. "Secured" = the counterparty *lacks the power* to foreclose.
"Power to foreclose" is a modal/dispositional fact — and whether a given power is one the
counterparty *may legitimately hold* re-imports the rights/legitimacy baseline
(is_any_primitive §1a). So **RC*, as a normative criterion, is not primitive either** — it
inherits the same circularity as everything else. The third horn is *not* reached by RC* as
a self-standing norm.

But here a real distinction finally bites, and it is the iteration's actual discovery.
Split RC* into two layers:

- **(D) The descriptive substrate.** *Who actually holds the power to foreclose whose
  exit?* This is a positive, mind-independent, **measurable** fact about the distribution
  of powers — preference-independent (it doesn't ask what she wants) and non-perfectionist
  (it doesn't rank lives). The third horn **exists at this layer**: the *shape of power* is
  a structural fact you can detect without subjective preference and without a theory of the
  good.
- **(N) The normative judgment.** *Is that distribution of power legitimate?* This still
  needs a baseline of which powers may be held — and that baseline is the contested,
  non-primitive web (rights/agency/interests). The third horn **does not exist at this
  layer**: legitimacy is not preference-independent-and-non-perfectionist; it is exactly the
  place the prior commitments must be paid.

The structural criterion the whole field has been groping for is real **as a measurement**
and unreal **as a complete normative theory.** It tells you the *shape* of domination, not
its *justice*.

## 5. The defensible niche this leaves FDK (precise, modest, not nothing)

This is where the iteration pays off, and it relocates FDK's contribution to a place it can
actually defend:

> **FDK is not a normative theory of legitimacy. It is the measurement instrument for the
> structural substrate (D) that Pettit, Sen, and Nozick all presuppose and none
> operationalised — a computable detector of "is exit secured / which party can foreclose
> whom" — over which a normative theory (supplied from outside) then renders the verdict
> (N).**

Every "circularity" that sank FDK as a *theory* is, for FDK as an *instrument*, a correctly
declared input: it measures the power-structure (D); it imports the legitimacy baseline (N)
exactly as a thermometer imports the question "is this fever dangerous?". The division of
labour with an enforcement layer falls out precisely: FDK computes the structural facts
about boundaries, consent tokens, and exit; the rights baseline (and the human owner)
supply N. This is the *same* "operational consent-checker" verdict as before — but iteration
2 gives it a philosophical *location* it did not have: **FDK occupies layer D, where the
preference-independent structural criterion really does exist; it never had a claim on layer
N, where it does not.** That is a smaller claim than "new paradigm" and a larger, more
precise one than "yet another libertarian formalisation." It is, finally, a true claim.

## 6. The iteration's verdict (and the next falsifiable step)

- **Killed harder:** FDK as a distinct *normative theory* is dead; RC* proves the reduction
  to Pettit by construction. The contented slave is handled only by *becoming* Pettit's
  modal account on one axis.
- **Saved precisely:** there is a preference-independent, non-perfectionist structural
  criterion — but it lives at the **descriptive** layer (the shape of power-to-foreclose),
  and FDK's real, defensible identity is as the **instrument that measures it**, not the
  theory that judges it.
- **The next test, and it is empirical, not philosophical:** can "exit-securedness" be
  given an actual **metric** (over real relations: employment, platforms, marriages,
  memberships), and does that metric predict third-party legitimacy judgments *better than
  stated satisfaction and better than occurrent exit*? If yes, FDK-as-instrument earns its
  keep and sharpens non-domination for everyone. If the metric tracks nothing satisfaction
  doesn't already track, even the instrument claim weakens. *That* is the experiment worth
  running — and it can lose.

So: not bullshit. A dead grand claim, an honest negative map, and one true small claim with
a falsifiable next step. In research, that is a good day's autopsy — it tells you precisely
what was alive and what to do next, which is more than most theories ever learn about
themselves.
