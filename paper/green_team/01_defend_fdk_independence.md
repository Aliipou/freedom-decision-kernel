projection of Nozick ∪ Pettit, silent where Sen alone speaks; 0 survivors across a

## 1. The strongest honest defense I can build

The reverse test's §5 challenge is the right target: one case with a clear verdict that
Nozick, Sen, **and** Pettit each get wrong or cannot reach, that FDK gets right, *without*
importing welfare / capability / autonomy / domination. Two routes are open: **(A)** find
that case; **(B)** show the test's method is rigged so that the case is wrongly disallowed.
I press both, hardest on the act-locus crack the red-team itself flagged as "the one place
FDK might be distinctive" (`red_team_the_verdict.md` §"locus of illegitimacy").

### 1A. The candidate case — the *abandoned irreversible protocol* with a present, identified victim

Construct to dodge every known sink at once:

> A founder deploys an autonomous smart-contract estate-lock: at t₀ Alice (competent,
> informed) co-signs an arrangement that, once the founder deletes their own keys,
> **binds Alice's ongoing access to her own assets to an immutable on-chain rule with no
> revision path and no controller.** At runtime: no agent holds any key (designer gone);
> Alice is materially well-provided by the rule (capabilities high); she consented at t₀.
> Years later Alice wants out. There is no door, and no one to open it.

- **Nozick — PERMIT.** Valid t₀ consent + valid transfer; no entitlement breached.
- **Sen — PERMIT.** Alice's capability set is intact, arguably enriched; no deprivation.
- **Pettit — strained toward PERMIT.** Domination is a *standing relation to an agent with
  capacity to interfere arbitrarily*. At runtime **there is no agent** — the keys are
  burned. Pettit must either stretch "domination" to authorless code (his own texts resist
  this: domination is *interpersonal*) or fall silent.
- **FDK — FORBID**, and seemingly on its *own* primitive: a present person (Alice, who
  exists and is identified — no non-identity escape) has her exit right alienated by an act
  that was not hers to authorize on her *future* self's behalf (`00_definitions.md`,
  Consent: revocability is necessary; Exit-right ≠ Exit-taken).

If this holds, FDK forbids where all three rivals permit-or-go-silent, **with a present
victim**, defeating the non-identity sink the killer test relied on. This is the single
strongest survivor candidate in the whole corpus.

### 1B. The method attack — is the "RELOCATES" verdict a rigged escape hatch?

The killer test kills every act-locus case with one move: when FDK alone forbids, it
declares the *reason* to be "ethics of irreversible institutional design (Buchanan/North),
not freedom," and files the case as **RELOCATES** rather than SURVIVES. This is the load-
bearing move, and it is **not** part of the §5 challenge as written. §5 asks only for a
case where FDK is *right* and the rivals are *wrong/silent* without importing the three
forbidden concepts. It does **not** require FDK's reason to be pre-certified as "about
freedom." The relocation move smuggles in a *fourth* gatekeeping criterion — "and the
reason must be freedom-shaped, by our lights" — that §5 never stated. If a referee accepts
that addition, *any* genuinely novel column can be dissolved by renaming its concern.
**That is the premature-closure risk, and it is real.** The defense's best shot is: §1A is
a survivor under §5 *as literally written*, and the kill depends on an unstated rule.

## 2. Why the defense fails anyway — three independent breaks

I wanted §1A to survive. It does not, and I have to be honest about all three failure
modes, each of which is sufficient on its own.

**Break 1 — the modal upgrade is Pettit, by construction.** For FDK to forbid §1A it must
read "exit foreclosed" *modally* (no door can be opened), not occurrently (Alice could
walk away from the assets and start over at some cost). But `iteration_secured_exit.md` §3
proves — not asserts, *proves by construction* — that the modal upgrade RC\* is a named
fragment of non-domination: illegitimacy relocated into "the counterparty's retained power
to shut the door" **is** Pettit's modal move. The instant I make Alice's case forbid, I
have made Pettit's move on her behalf, and I no longer get to say Pettit is silent. The
"no agent at runtime" dodge does not save me: if there is genuinely no power-holder, then
there is no *retained power* either, and FDK's modal forbid loses the very thing it indexes
on. FDK forbids *only* when there is a foreclosing power — and that is exactly when Pettit
speaks. **The silence of Pettit and the firing of FDK are mutually exclusive.** This is the
deepest point and I cannot get around it.

**Break 2 — Rothbard, who is in the rival set, closes the personal version.** Where §1A is
genuinely about Alice's *person/will* (not external property), FDK's forbid is precisely
Rothbard's inalienability-of-will: you cannot validly bind your *person* irrevocably, though
you *may* irrevocably transfer external *things* (`red_team_the_verdict.md` §3). FDK draws
the same person/thing partition under a consent-shaped name. The §5 challenge names Nozick,
Sen, Pettit — but Rothbard is squarely in FDK's own enemy dossier (`01_how_rothbard_kills_
fdk.md`), so "distinctive against the named three but identical to Rothbard" is still
*projection of the rivals*, not an independent column.

**Break 3 — the relocation move, on inspection, is not rigged.** I wanted §1B to be the
escape. It is not, and the reason is clean. To make Pettit *and* Rothbard *both* silent, I
am forced (Break 1 + Break 2) to strip the case of any power-holder **and** any alienated
*person* — i.e. make the foreclosed thing impersonal and inert (pure code, no will, no
agent). At that exact point Alice is no longer the locus of the wrong: there is no boundary
of *hers* being crossed by anyone, because "crossing" in FDK's ontology (`00_definitions.md`,
Legitimacy = an action crossing a boundary with valid consent of its owner) requires an
*actor* whose act it is. An authorless rule has no actor at runtime. So FDK's own gate has
**no clean actor-and-boundary to fire on** — it goes silent too, or it must relocate the
wrong from "Alice's boundary" to "the system cannot be re-constituted," which is not its
primitive. The relocation verdict is therefore **forced by FDK's own definitions**, not
imposed from outside. §1B's "unstated fourth criterion" objection dissolves: the criterion
*is* stated — it is FDK's own actor-indexed definition of a boundary crossing. The killer
test was reading FDK's ontology back to it, not rigging a taxonomy.

## 3. Did the test miss a fourth dimension? (the honest residual)

The most I can salvage is the red-team's own admission: there *is* a live conceptual
**question** — *is the act of designing future exit-foreclosure itself a site of
evaluation, distinct from any runtime relation?* The killer test concedes this is real and
"possibly novel" — but relocates it to mechanism-design / constitutional-economics
theorists, not freedom theorists. I pressed whether that relocation is premature. It is
not: every attempt to keep the question *inside* freedom theory re-collides with Break 1
(a present power-holder → Pettit) or Break 2 (an alienated will → Rothbard), and every
attempt to keep the rivals silent collides with Break 3 (no actor → FDK silent / wrong is
about a design's irreversibility). The three breaks form a closed trap with no interior
seat. That is a *convergent mechanistic* closure, not an enumeration — which is precisely
what raises a "0/N" from suggestive to robust. The red-team did not close prematurely; it
closed with a reason.

## 4. Adjudication

I tried the strongest case (§1A) and the strongest method attack (§1B). Both fail, and they
fail to *three independent, mutually reinforcing* breaks — the modal upgrade is Pettit by
construction, the personal residue is Rothbard, and the impersonal residue silences FDK's
own actor-indexed gate. The relocation verdict the red-team leans on is not a rigged escape
hatch; it is forced by FDK's own definition of a boundary crossing. There is no surviving
case and no genuine methodological flaw. The one thing that survives — *is authored future
exit-foreclosure a distinct site of evaluation?* — is a real question, but it is not a
freedom-theory verdict-column, exactly as the red-team said. The burden remains, correctly,
on anyone claiming a survivor; I could not discharge it, and I now see the structural reason
no one can on freedom's terms.

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED.** The red-team is robust. FDK does not produce an independent
verdict-column; its verdicts project onto Nozick ∪ Pettit (and Rothbard), and it falls
silent exactly where Sen alone speaks. The kill was not premature.
