# Green Team: Defending "Consent Is Foundational" and "The Revocation Condition Is Derivable from Consent Alone"

*Mandate: mount the strongest honest defense of two killed claims and check for premature
closure. (a) Consent is a foundational normative concept (red-team:
[`../consent_not_foundational.md`](../consent_not_foundational.md),
[`../is_any_primitive.md`](../is_any_primitive.md)). (b) The Revocation Condition is derivable
from the concept of consent alone (red-team: [`../three_questions.md`](../three_questions.md),
Q2). No manufactured defense. A failed defense that confirms the kill is the honest, expected
result.*

## Claim (a): Is there a primitive-yet-adequate notion of consent?

The red-team's kill is the thin/thick dilemma: thin consent (bare "yes") is primitive but
monstrous; thick consent (the agent's own, competent, uncoerced, informed) is adequate but
re-imports autonomy + competence + non-domination + capability + property. There is alleged
to be no third notion that is both primitive and adequate.

**The strongest honest defense — consent as a primitive normative power (Raz/Owens).** The
defense does not try to rescue *thin* consent. It denies the dilemma is exhaustive by locating
a third notion: consent as the *exercise of a normative power* — a sui generis capacity to
alter the deontic landscape by an act of will (Raz, *The Morality of Freedom*; Owens, *Shaping
the Normative Landscape*; Hohfeld's power-incident). On this view "consent" names neither a raw
psychological "yes" (thin) nor a "yes" decorated with five external theories (thick), but a
*single* primitive: the will's authority to change what others may do to one. The wager is that
this primitive does normative work *before* and *independently of* the five "upstream" concepts
— that those concepts are not inputs the power presupposes but *internal articulations of the
power's own success conditions*.

**Does it beat the red-team? Engaging each import.**

- *Autonomy.* Owens' best move: the capacity to bind oneself by one's word does not
  presuppose a Frankfurt/Christman theory of authentic preference; it presupposes only that
  there *is* a will whose say-so counts. "Whose will counts" can be read as constitutive of the
  power, not imported into it. — **But** the red-team's point (§1) is sharper than "there must be
  a will": it is that distinguishing a *consenting* yes from a conditioned/manipulated yes
  requires a criterion of authorship, and the normative-power story is silent on where that line
  falls. Owens can say the power exists; he cannot, from the power alone, say the hypnotized
  subject failed to exercise it. The line is still imported. *Import survives.*

- *Competence.* This is the cleanest concession in the red-team and the defense cannot
  dislodge it: FDK's *own architecture* treats authority/competence as a **separate axis the
  gate reads** ([`../../spec/frontiers/competence_spectrum.md`](../../spec/frontiers/competence_spectrum.md)).
  A normative power presupposes a power-*holder* of sufficient standing; "sufficient" is not
  set by the concept of the power. *Import survives — and is conceded in the design.*

- *Coercion / non-domination.* The deepest import (§3). Even granting consent is a primitive
  power, *whether a given act was a free exercise of it* turns on a moralised baseline (Nozick,
  Wertheimer): "your money or your life" voids the waiver, "this price or no deal" does not, and
  only a prior entitlement-allocation separates them. The normative-power framework is wholly
  silent on the baseline. *Import survives — and it is the rival (Pettit) sitting underneath.*

- *Capability + disclosure.* "Informed over a real option" needs a duty-of-disclosure theory
  and a Senian capability theory to keep "real option" from collapsing into "formally
  available." The power framework gives neither. *Import survives.*

**Engaging Kant/Scanlon/Habermas (does any give consent genuine primitiveness?).** No — and
they are the defense's *own* tradition turning against it. Each took bare agreement to be inert
and *thickened* it with a prior structure: Kant's contract is an *idea of reason* (the moral law
does the work), Scanlon's "reasonable rejection" imports a whole theory of reasons and burdens,
Habermas's agreement only *registers* validity conferred by the ideal-speech conditions (an
anti-domination baseline). Two-plus centuries of the strongest contractualists converge on the
concession that consent must be enriched to be adequate, and every enrichment is a prior
commitment. The defense cannot recruit them; they are evidence *for* the kill.

**The one genuine gain the defense secures — and why it is not enough.** The normative-power
literature *does* establish something real: consent is not *reducible to* any one of the five
imports. It is an irreducible *power* whose exercise those concepts condition. This blocks the
crude reduction "consent just *is* autonomy" or "just *is* non-domination." But foundationality
requires more than irreducibility: it requires that the concept's *normative adequacy be
generated, not borrowed*. A primitive that is real-but-conditioned-from-outside is exactly the
red-team's verdict: **a node, not a root.** Being an irreducible power and being a *foundation*
come apart, and the defense secures only the first. The "primitive-yet-adequate" notion the
rescue needs — primitive in source *and* adequate in verdicts — is not exhibited. The
normative-power power is primitive in *kind* but not *adequate alone*; thick consent is adequate
but not primitive in *source*. The gap the red-team named is not closed; it is relocated and
confirmed.

**Symmetry note (does [`../is_any_primitive.md`](../is_any_primitive.md) rescue (a)?).** That
paper shows the rivals (non-domination, capability, self-ownership) are *also* non-primitive — a
web with no floor. This is real and it does demote the *indictment*: non-foundationalism is the
universal condition, not FDK's peculiar flaw. But it does **not** overturn (a). "No one is
foundational" is not "consent is foundational." The honest yield is: consent is *no worse* than
its rivals on foundationality, not that it *is* a foundation. Claim (a), read literally, still
fails.

## Claim (b): Is RC derivable from the concept of consent ALONE?

The candidate derivation (Q2's own strongest form): (1) consenting is exercising a normative
power; (2) revoking is re-exercising it; (3) an irrevocable arrangement uses the power to
annihilate the power; (4) a power cannot license its own annihilation; (5) therefore consent to
irrevocability is invalid — RC follows. The kill is the **overgeneration objection** at step 4:
ordinary transfers (selling a car, waiving a debt) *are* legitimately irrevocable, so the line
between *alienable transfer* and *inalienable exit* must be drawn — and drawing it allegedly
requires importing a person-premise (self-ownership / autonomy / non-domination).

**The strongest honest defense — the reflexivity line, drawn from the structure of powers
alone.** The defense's best shot is that the alienable/inalienable line is *not* a substantive
fact about the special metaphysical status of the person, but a **formal/structural** fact about
the *logic of normative powers* — and is therefore available from "the concept of consent"
construed as "the concept of a normative power," with no person-premise.

The distinction: selling a car alienates a *first-order claim* over an *external object*. The
power that did the alienating *survives the transaction* — having sold the car, I retain my full
capacity to consent, transfer, and waive. By contrast, consenting to irrevocable servitude
purports to alienate the *power itself*: it uses the consent-power to extinguish the standing to
re-exercise the consent-power. The first is a power *operating on an object*; the second is a
power *operating to destroy itself*. Step 4 ("a power cannot license its own annihilation") then
fires on the second case but *not* the first — and the line is drawn by a purely reflexive,
second-order criterion: **does the exercise leave the power intact (object-transfer) or
self-undermine it (power-transfer)?** No claim that "the person is special" is needed; only the
formal observation that a self-annihilating exercise of a power is not a valid exercise *of that
power*, on pain of pragmatic self-contradiction (the entrenchment puzzle: a legislature cannot
bind its successors; one cannot promise away the capacity to promise).

**This is the best move available, and it is genuinely better than the red-team credited.** The
red-team's Q2 asserts the line "cannot be drawn from the concept of consent alone" and *jumps*
to the person-premise menu. But the reflexive criterion (object-alienation vs power-alienation)
*is* drawable from the concept of a normative power, and it does sort the paradigm cases
correctly: car/debt (power survives) → valid; voluntary slavery (power destroyed) → invalid.
For these cases there is a real structural derivation owing nothing to Rothbard, Mill, or
Pettit. **This is the strongest honest case for overturning Q2, and it must be stated at full
strength before it is tested.**

**Where it breaks — the test that decides it.** The reflexive line holds only if "alienating the
power itself" can be identified *without* a prior theory of which holdings are constitutive of
the agent vs external to her. Three pressures, each fatal:

1. **The boundary between object and power is not power-internal.** Is my *labor for life*
   an external object I may alienate (like the car) or part of the power-bearing self I may not?
   Is a 30-year non-compete? Lifetime exclusive personal service? Selling an organ? The
   reflexive criterion *presupposes* an answer to "what is the power, as against its objects" —
   and that demarcation of the self's constitutive holdings from its external holdings *is* the
   person-premise (self-ownership/autonomy) in precise form. The defense smuggles it into the
   word "itself" in "annihilate the power *itself*." **Import survives, disguised as syntax.**

2. **Object-transfers can be permanently agency-foreclosing, and power-transfers need not
   be.** Selling all my assets into permanent destitution leaves the consent-*power* formally
   intact yet may foreclose every real future exercise of it (a capability point); conversely
   some "self-binding" (a Ulysses contract, a binding arbitration clause) constrains future
   exercise without annihilating the power. To say the first is permissible (formal power
   intact) and the second sometimes impermissible, the defense must rank *which* foreclosures
   matter — and "which foreclosures of future agency count" is the autonomy/capability premise,
   not a formal fact. **Import survives.**

3. **The retained-exit contented slave (Q3) defeats it even where it fires.** Where exit *is*
   preserved, the reflexive argument does not fire at all (the power is not annihilated), so RC
   must return VALID — i.e. Rothbard — unless it adds that her unwanted exit is "not real *for
   her*," which is Sen's adaptive-preference (a capability/autonomy import). The structural line
   is silent exactly where the hard case lives.

**Verdict on the line.** The reflexive criterion is a *real* and underrated partial result: for
clean object-vs-self cases it derives RC from power-structure with no person-premise, and the
red-team understated this. But it cannot draw the *general* alienable/inalienable boundary,
because identifying "the power itself" — which holdings are constitutive of the agent — *is* the
imported premise about the special status of the person, now hiding inside the reflexive
predicate rather than stated openly. The derivation is not from consent *alone*; it is from
consent *plus a demarcation of the self*, which is self-ownership/autonomy by another name. Q2's
kill stands, though it should be amended to credit the reflexive partial-derivation and to
locate the import more precisely (in the individuation of the power, not as a freestanding extra
axiom).

## Honest adjudication

**Claim (a) — "Consent is a foundational normative concept": CONFIRMED (kill stands).** The
strongest honest defense (Raz/Owens normative-power primitive) secures that consent is
*irreducible* — a genuine sui generis power, not collapsible into any single rival — but
foundationality needs *generated* adequacy, and every gram of adequacy is still borrowed
(autonomy/competence/coercion-baseline/capability/property). Kant, Scanlon, and Habermas
corroborate the kill rather than the rescue. The symmetric-circularity paper demotes the
indictment (no rival is foundational either) but does not promote consent to a root. *No
premature closure: the red-team is correct that consent is a node.* The one honest amendment
the defense earns: the red-team may slightly over-claim by sliding from "not foundational" to
"borrowed at every joint" — consent is *irreducible-but-conditioned*, which is a node with real
structure, not a mere projection.

**Claim (b) — "RC is derivable from consent ALONE": CONFIRMED (kill stands), with a credited
partial-derivation.** The reflexive line (object-alienation leaves the power intact;
power-alienation self-annihilates) is a real structural derivation that the red-team
*understated* — for clean cases it yields RC with no person-premise. But the general
alienable/inalienable boundary requires individuating "the power itself" from its objects, and
that demarcation of the self's constitutive holdings *is* the self-ownership/autonomy premise,
relocated into the reflexive predicate rather than eliminated. Q2's "needs an imported
person-premise" holds. *No premature closure on the verdict — but the red-team should be amended
to acknowledge the reflexive partial-derivation and to name the import precisely as the
individuation of the power.*

Neither claim is OVERTURNED. Both kills are CONFIRMED. The defense's genuine residue is two
sharpenings, not a reversal: (a) consent is an *irreducible-but-conditioned* power (node with
structure), and (b) RC is *partially* derivable from power-reflexivity for object-vs-self cases,
with the person-premise entering precisely at the individuation of the power.
