# H2, Three Ways — and Do Not Reify "Reversibility"

*The philosophical contest is closed. What remains is to be precise about the three things
H2 could mean, to name the prior occupants honestly (there are two, not one), and to refuse
the temptation to assume FDK measures "reversibility" when it might measure something else
entirely. This is a short paper because argument has nearly run out of work to do; the rest
is data.*

*Theory: نظریه آزادی (Theory of Freedom), Mohammad Ali Jannat Khah Doust (CC BY 4.0).*

---

## 1. Two prior occupants, not one

The reframe's danger is re-invention, and it is worse than a single 1974 paper:

- **Quasi-option value** — Arrow & Fisher (1974), Henry (1974), generalised by Dixit &
  Pindyck (1994): the value of *not* making an irreversible decision under uncertainty. This
  is the prior occupant of the **reversibility** flavour of H2.
- **Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970)** — the prior occupant of the **exit**
  flavour, and arguably the more on-the-nose: exit is a structural variable; the
  *availability* of exit changes a system's behaviour; *blocked* exit produces distinct
  institutional dynamics (voice, decline, capture). FDK's "preserved exit right," read as a
  structural property of institutions, is squarely Hirschman's territory.

So H2 is flanked: its reversibility face is quasi-option value; its exit face is Hirschman.
Whichever way FDK leans, a major prior framework is already there. This does not refute H2 —
but it sets the novelty bar exactly where §3 puts it.

## 2. The three readings of H2 (the decision structure)

| Reading | Claim | Novelty | Likely status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H2-A — Rename** | FDK = reversibility = the existing lock-in / exit literature | ≈ 0 | **modal verdict on novelty** |
| **H2-B — Operationalisation** | No new *idea*, but a *computable, formalised, machine-readable* index deployable over real consent graphs | medium–high (engineering-tool value) | **probably already true** |
| **H2-C — Incremental prediction** | FDK predicts institutional failure that Hirschman, Arthur, switching-cost, and optionality measures **cannot** (ΔR² > 0) | high — genuine science | **unproven; the only open shot** |

The honest current bet: **H2-B is true today** (FDK *is* a deployable computation, and that
is what the product/engineering story already banks — independent of philosophy). **H2-A is
the most likely verdict on novelty.** **H2-C is the live, unproven possibility**, and the
only one that would make FDK a scientific contribution rather than a useful re-implementation.

## 3. Do not reify "reversibility" (the latent variable is itself unknown)

The deepest correction: *"FDK measures reversibility"* is the current best hypothesis about
FDK's own latent variable — **not a result.** After serious empirical work it may turn out
that FDK's score actually tracks:

- **switching cost** (IO economics), or
- **option value** (real options), or
- **recovery cost** (resilience engineering), or
- **foreclosure risk** (the probability exit becomes unavailable), or
- **dependency depth** (how many irreversible commitments a state rests on), or
- some **composite** of these.

So the empirical program must **not assume the latent** and then "confirm" it. It must
*identify* it: compute the FDK index on real arrangements, measure each candidate construct
independently, and ask — by factor analysis / which constructs FDK loads on — *what is FDK
actually a proxy for?* "FDK = reversibility" is a hypothesis about FDK's own meaning, to be
tested alongside the prediction. The project has, for the first time, a question about
itself that data can answer.

## 4. The next artifact is not philosophy

The next document should not contain the words *freedom*, *consent*, or *legitimacy*. It
should be titled something like:

> **"Does the FDK Reversibility Index Predict Institutional Failure Beyond Existing Lock-In
> Measures?"**

and it requires two things that are *build/fieldwork*, not argument: (i) **define and
compute the index** as a number in [0,1] over a consent/ownership graph (the one piece of
engineering still worth doing — it is the experiment's apparatus, not another layer); and
(ii) **regress real failure outcomes** (protocol/platform collapse, constitutional crisis,
currency-union breakup, institutional dissolution) on the established predictors **plus** the
FDK index, and report ΔR². Absorbed if ≈ 0; alive if robustly > 0.

## 5. Where argument stops

This is the edge of what reasoning from the armchair can settle. Eleven critical papers have
established: H1 (a new theory of freedom) is dead; FDK is at best a *consent-indexed,
computable structural index*; that index is flanked by quasi-option value and Hirschman; and
its independence now reduces to a single measurable quantity, ΔR², over a latent variable
that is itself an empirical question. No paper #13 about freedom will move any of this. The
honest next move is to **build the index and get the data** — and to hold, until then, the
exact and unflattering status: *defensible engineering instrument (H2-B), novelty unproven
and most-likely-absorbed (H2-A), genuine-discovery possible but untested (H2-C).*

That the project can state its own status this precisely — and that the status is falsifiable
rather than rhetorical — is the thing most theory-building never achieves. It is no longer
defending an idea; it is measuring one.
