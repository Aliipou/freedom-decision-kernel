# How Rawls Kills FDK

> Phase 2 of the Exit-Right Program: write the strongest attack a real Rawlsian
> would mount, in his own terms, before deciding whether FDK survives it. The brief
> is adversarial *and* honest. We steelman *A Theory of Justice* (1971) and
> *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement* (2001) at full strength, then find where FDK
> actually breaks — not where it is merely inconvenienced. Outcomes admitted in
> advance: survive / partial-collapse / collapse. The verdict below is partial
> collapse, and it is conceded plainly.

---

## 1. Rawls at full strength (no strawman)

Rawls is not a welfarist and not a libertarian, and FDK must not file him under
either. His commitments, stated faithfully:

- **Justice as fairness.** Principles of justice are whatever free and equal
  persons would agree to in a fair initial choice situation. Legitimacy flows from
  the *fairness of the procedure that selects the principles*, not from outcomes
  and not from a prior natural right to holdings.
- **The original position and the veil of ignorance.** Parties choose principles
  not knowing their place, class, talents, conception of the good, or generation.
  The veil strips exactly the information that lets the strong write rules favoring
  the strong. It is *constructive*: it does not discover a pre-existing moral fact
  (a Lockean title), it *builds* fairness from the symmetry of the choosers.
- **The two principles, in lexical order.**
  1. Each person has an equal claim to a fully adequate scheme of **equal basic
     liberties**, compatible with the same scheme for all (liberty has priority).
  2. Social and economic inequalities are just only if (a) attached to offices
     open under **fair equality of opportunity**, and (b) to the **greatest benefit
     of the least advantaged** (the difference principle).
- **The basic structure is the subject of justice.** The primary object of justice
  is not individual transactions but the **basic structure**: the major
  institutions (the constitution, the economy, property law, the family) that
  distribute fundamental rights, shape life chances *before anyone acts*, and whose
  effects are "profound and present from the start." Justice is assessed at the
  level of the rules of the game, not move by move.
- **Pure procedural justice vs. background justice.** A procedure is *purely*
  procedural only when its background is already just. A fair bet at a fair table
  needs no separate check on the outcome; a fair-looking bet at a rigged table
  launders the rigging. Rawls's deepest move: **the fairness of any particular
  transaction is parasitic on the justice of the background structure that set the
  parties' starting positions and options.** Local consent cannot certify itself.
- **The just savings principle.** Parties behind the veil do not know their
  generation. They therefore agree to a rate of **just savings** — a duty owed
  *across* generations to maintain just institutions and not exhaust the world.
  This is generated *without* identifying particular future persons; it binds the
  present generation as such.

Two of these land directly on FDK's throat.

## 2. The two kills

### Kill A — FDK has no theory of the background, so its "consent" certifies the rigging

FDK's gate asks one question: does every boundary this action crosses have the
valid consent of that boundary's owner? To ask it, FDK must **take the ownership
graph as given** — who owns what, who stands where, what each party's feasible
options are. The kernel audits transactions *against that baseline* and never
audits the baseline. `00_definitions.md` concedes this twice: origin legitimacy is
"the Ownership-Genesis hole, off this axis," and Freedom's separation note flags as
[UNDERDETERMINED] "whether option-poverty produced by others' prior legitimate acts
counts as a boundary crossing."

Rawls's strike: **that is not a gap at the edge of FDK; it is the whole of what
justice is about, and FDK has placed it out of scope.** Consider the company-town
worker (RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION C2; consent_authenticity §2.1). She signs each
transaction freely; no single act removes her exit; her consent is informed,
specific, competent, voluntary, revocable. FDK's gate — *even with the exit-right
condition firing on the closed loop* — at best catches the lock-in of *this*
arrangement. It cannot reach the prior fact that **the basic structure left her no
adequate alternative to entering some such arrangement at all**: no public
education, no fair opportunity, no exit *into* a decent option, a property regime
that concentrated the means of livelihood before she was born. Her "yes" is a fair
bet at a rigged table. FDK certifies the bet because FDK cannot see the table.

This is sharper than the welfare objection, and FDK cannot deflect it the way it
deflects Sen. FDK's standard reply — "we refuse to read welfare; we only read
consent" — does not touch Rawls, because **he is not asking FDK to read welfare; he
is asking it to read the justice of the option set, which is a question about
liberties and opportunities, not satisfaction.** Fair equality of opportunity is a
structural condition on the rules, not a welfare metric, so FDK's anti-welfare
spine gives it no immunity. The worker's consent is procedurally clean and morally
void *for a structural reason FDK's own vocabulary cannot express*: the relevant
boundary was drawn, and the relevant exits foreclosed, by the basic structure,
upstream of every transaction the gate can see.

The exit-right program is FDK's best answer, and Rawls turns it. Exit-right asks:
can she leave *this* arrangement? Rawls asks: **into what?** An exit that delivers
her to a structurally foreclosed feasible set is, in Rawls's terms, no real
liberty — not because she is unhappy, but because the *background* never secured
the equal basic liberty and fair opportunity that make the choice a free one.
FDK either (i) stays silent on "into what?" — and then certifies the company town
exactly as Rothbard does, *minus* a theory of why the baseline is acceptable — or
(ii) starts auditing the justice of the option set — and then it is doing Rawlsian
background justice, with no apparatus of its own to do it. There is no third door
that keeps FDK's consent-only architecture *and* answers the question.

### Kill B — Just savings handles future generations crisply, where FDK collapses

RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION S3 already concedes the wound: on Parfit's non-identity
problem (a present act that lowers the quality of a future generation *that would
not otherwise exist*), FDK returns ALLOW / silent — "a victim-indexed,
consent-indexed gate cannot represent a victimless duty." There is no present
boundary-owner who failed to consent; the future persons are not identifiable
victims; the act is a *condition of their existence*. FDK's gate has nothing to
fire on.

Rawls is **crisp exactly here, and by construction.** The just savings principle is
generated behind the veil precisely *because* the parties do not know their
generation — so it never needs to name a future person to bind the present one. It
is a duty owed by the present generation *as such* to maintain just institutions
across time. Non-identity is fatal to any consent-indexed or victim-indexed theory
(FDK, and Rothbard with it); it is **harmless** to Rawls, because his duty is
indexed to *institutions across generations*, not to *consenting parties*. Where
FDK is structurally blind, Rawls has a principle that was designed for this case
before Parfit named it.

This is not a place FDK is merely "incomplete." It is a place where a rival is
clear and FDK is *worse*: silent where a duty plainly exists, and silent for a
reason internal to its architecture (consent needs a present owner; the future has
none). The intergenerational domain is one where the empty chair is not empty —
Rawls already sits in it.

## 3. The death conditions, stated as falsifiers

> **Death condition A (background justice).** *If* the moral force of consent
> depends on the justice of the background structure that fixed the parties'
> positions and options — *and* a transaction certified by FDK can be illegitimate
> solely because that background was unjust, with nothing in the transaction itself
> defective — *then* FDK's transaction-level consent gate cannot be a theory of
> legitimacy. It certifies "free" choices against an unexamined, possibly unjust
> baseline. **If background justice is prior to consent, FDK is false** as a
> standalone account: at most it is the *downstream* module of a theory whose
> upstream — the part that does the justifying — is missing.

> **Death condition B (intergenerational duty).** *If* there exist binding duties
> to future generations that hold without an identifiable, presently-consenting
> victim (just savings; non-identity cases) — *then* a victim-indexed,
> consent-indexed gate cannot represent them, and **FDK is false as a complete
> theory of legitimacy across time.** It is silent where the duty is real.

Both falsifiers are *constructive*: they tell FDK exactly what to exhibit to
survive — an account of when a baseline is unjust *that does not read welfare*
(for A), and a victimless, consent-free duty its gate can represent (for B). FDK
can produce neither without ceasing to be FDK.

## 4. Honest verdict: PARTIAL COLLAPSE (closer to collapse on B)

Not total collapse — and the limits of Rawls's kill must be stated as plainly as
its force.

**Where FDK survives the Rawlsian.** Rawls is famously *underdetermined at the
transaction*. The basic structure being just does not tell you whether *this*
lifetime-slavery contract or *this* lock-in is legitimate; Rawls's own machinery
operates on institutions, and "mixed" is the honest entry under Rawls in
EXIT_RIGHT_PROGRAM's Phase-3 table for exactly the marginal cases FDK targets.
Here FDK has a genuine, *complementary* contribution: a determinate,
non-welfarist, structural test (the preserved exit/revocation right, T2–T4) for the
*individual* arrangement, which Rawls's institution-level theory does not deliver.
A defensible division of labor exists — Rawls owns the basic structure, FDK owns
the transaction *given* a structure — and on that reading FDK is **distinct, not
redundant**, on its own axis. The Rawlsian cannot derive FDK's lifetime-slavery
DENY from the difference principle; FDK can.

**Where FDK partially collapses (Kill A).** That division of labor is a demotion,
and an honest one. It concedes Rawls's central thesis: FDK is *not* a freestanding
theory of legitimacy, because legitimacy is not freestanding from background
justice. FDK becomes a **conditional** instrument — "given a just-enough baseline,
here is the transaction test" — and it *cannot certify its own antecedent*. Every
FDK ALLOW carries a silent "assuming the ownership graph is just," and FDK has, by
its own admission, no theory of when that holds. That is a real loss of scope, not
a rhetorical one. The empty chair "protection without paternalism" is still
*occupiable at the transaction level*, but only inside a room Rawls built.

**Where FDK approaches full collapse (Kill B).** On intergenerational duty there
is no division of labor to retreat to. FDK is not underdetermined here; it is
*structurally incapable* — its gate cannot represent a duty with no present
consenting owner, and just savings shows the duty is real and crisply groundable.
This is the harder wound. It is also off FDK's chosen axis (the exit-right program
is about *present* consent and lock-in), so it bounds FDK's domain rather than
refuting the axis itself — but within its scope as a *general* legitimacy theory,
B is a flat defeat.

The combined reading: **FDK is not killed as a transaction-level consent test;
it is killed as a complete, standalone theory of justice.** Rawls forces the
demotion from "theory of legitimacy" to "transaction module conditional on a
background it cannot supply, silent on the intergenerational duties it cannot
index." That is partial collapse — survival of the distinctive axis, loss of the
imperial claim.

## 5. Redundant or distinct on this axis?

**Distinct — but subordinate.** On the *background-structure* axis, FDK is not a
rival to Rawls at all; it has no entry, and pretending otherwise would be the
strawman this file refuses. On the *transaction* axis, FDK is genuinely distinct
from Rawls: it issues determinate, non-welfarist verdicts on individual lock-in /
lifetime-slavery cases where Rawls is "mixed." The two theories are therefore
**complementary, not redundant** — Rawls justifies the table, FDK adjudicates the
bet — *but the complementarity is asymmetric*: FDK needs Rawls (it cannot certify
its baseline), and Rawls does not need FDK (he can leave the transaction
underdetermined and lose little). A junior partner is still distinct from its
senior. It is not, on its own, the firm.

The one move that would *upgrade* FDK from junior partner to genuine rival is the
move it has not made and may be unable to make: an account of an **unjust baseline
that is detectable structurally, without reading welfare** — i.e., a structural
theory of when the option set itself is illegitimate (option-poverty as a boundary
crossing, the [UNDERDETERMINED] flag in Freedom's definition). If that exists, FDK
answers Kill A on its own terms and reclaims the chair. If it does not — if every
attempt to say "this baseline is unjust" smuggles in either welfare (becoming Sen)
or a prior natural title (becoming Rothbard/Nozick, and inheriting their
unanswered rectification problem) — then Rawls's demotion stands, and FDK's home
is the transaction, on loan from a landlord it cannot pay.
