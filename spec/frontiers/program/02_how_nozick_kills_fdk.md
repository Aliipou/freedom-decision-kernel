# How Nozick kills FDK — the rectification void and the pattern that liberty upsets

> Phase 2 of the Exit-Right Program. Nozick is the more dangerous rival than
> Rothbard, because he attacks from **two opposite directions at once**: he shows
> FDK does *too little* (it has no rectification principle, so it launders historical
> injustice — the under-protection charge) *and* that FDK's one original move does
> *too much* (mandatory revocability is itself a forbidden pattern that "liberty
> upsets" — the over-reach charge). A theory can survive being called too weak or
> too strong. Surviving both, from the same critic, is the test. Three outcomes are
> admissible: survive, partially survive, die.

## 1. The strongest Nozick (steelman, his actual commitments)

- **Side-constraints, not goals.** Rights are not values to be maximized; they are
  *constraints* on what may be done to persons, grounded in the Kantian separateness
  of persons — you may not use one person as a means for others' ends, *even to
  minimize total rights-violations*. This is precisely FDK's anti-aggregation spine:
  FDK refuses to cross a non-consenting boundary even to raise welfare. **FDK's core
  is a side-constraint theory.** That shared DNA is what makes Nozick's divergences
  so sharp: he is family, and the family quarrel is over the parts FDK left out.
- **The entitlement theory — three parts, and the third is decisive.** A holding is
  just iff it arises by (1) **just original acquisition**, (2) **just transfer**, or
  (3) **rectification** of violations of (1) and (2). A distribution is just iff it
  *descends by just steps from a just start* — history, not pattern. **FDK
  implements (1) and (2) and is silent on (3).** This is not a minor omission; for
  Nozick the principle of rectification is a *constitutive third pillar*, without
  which the entitlement theory cannot evaluate any real holding, because no real
  holding has a clean history.
- **The principle of rectification — and his candor about it.** Nozick *admitted he
  could not specify it*: what is owed when the chain of transfers contains a theft
  generations back? He gestured at it (possibly the difference principle as a
  rough-and-ready rectificatory rule, since the worst-off are the probable
  descendants of the expropriated) and left it open. **This is not a weakness FDK
  can exploit — it is a weakness FDK shares and made worse**, because FDK does not
  even *gesture*. Nozick at least names the hole; FDK's clean-chain audit walks past
  it (O1).
- **Wilt Chamberlain — "liberty upsets patterns."** Start from any patterned just
  distribution D1 (equality, need, desert — whatever you like). A million fans each
  freely pay Wilt a quarter to watch him. The result D2 is unequal. Either D2 is
  just (and your pattern is dead, because *liberty produced it from voluntary
  transfers*) or you must **continually interfere** with people's voluntary
  exchanges to preserve the pattern — forbidding capitalist acts between consenting
  adults. **Any end-state or patterned principle requires perpetual coercion to
  maintain.** This is Nozick's most portable weapon, and §2 turns it on FDK itself.
- **The experience machine.** Offered a tank that guarantees any experiences you
  want, you decline — because we care about *actually doing* things and *being* a
  certain person, not about experiential states. This is an argument *against
  welfare/experiential metrics as the measure of the good*, and FDK quotes it
  approvingly (Freedom ≠ Welfare ≠ Happiness). But notice it is also an argument
  that **what matters is the exercise of agency over a real life** — which, in §2,
  cuts toward Sen and against FDK's purely structural test.

Nozick is FDK's closest kin: same side-constraint backbone, same anti-welfarism,
same suspicion of patterns. That kinship is exactly why his two kills land — they
come from *inside* FDK's own commitments, not from a rival framework FDK can dismiss.

## 2. The sharpest attack — two kills from one critic

### Kill A — the rectification void: FDK launders historical injustice

FDK validates *transfers* and audits *no origin* (O1, O2). Its title is "an unbroken
consent-grounded chain from a legitimate origin" — but it has **no procedure to test
the origin** (Genesis is flagged open) and, decisively, **no procedure to repair a
chain that began in or passed through a violation.** Nozick's charge:

> *Your kernel will issue a confident ALLOW to the present holder of land seized by
> conquest three centuries ago, and a confident DENY to the dispossessed heir's
> reclaim — because every* transfer *since the theft was consensual. You have built
> a machine that **certifies the proceeds of aggression as legitimate** the instant
> the original victim is dead and the title has been sold once. That is not
> neutrality; it is **laundering**, and it is exactly the failure I flagged in
> myself and could not fix — except I at least knew the third pillar was load-
> bearing and missing. You removed it and declared the building sound.*

This is sharper than the Rothbard version because Rothbard *has* a remedy
(return to identifiable descendants); FDK has neither Rothbard's original-title
remedy nor Nozick's gestured rectification. FDK's verdict on O1 is therefore not
"consent-purist" — it is **incoherent by its own definition**, which requires a
"legitimate origin" the kernel never checks. A consent-grounded chain from an
*illegitimate* origin is, on FDK's own terms, not legitimate; yet FDK ALLOWs it. The
kernel's silence is not abstention; it is a positive ALLOW that contradicts the
definition of title it claims to enforce.

### Kill B — mandatory revocability is a pattern that liberty upsets

Now Nozick turns Chamberlain on FDK's one original move. T2 says: *no valid consent
may foreclose its own revocation; the exit right is not the owner's to alienate.*
Reconstruct Chamberlain:

> *Start from a distribution of rights you call just: each person fully self-owning.
> Now a person, fully informed and competent, voluntarily enters an arrangement
> that forecloses their own future exit — a binding lifetime commitment, a covenant,
> an irrevocable pledge. This is a **capitalist act between consenting adults**: a
> voluntary transfer from a just starting point. FDK steps in and says DENY — the
> arrangement is illegitimate to enter. But on what authority? You have just done
> the thing I proved no one may do: you have a **patterned principle** ("all
> rights-holdings must preserve an open exit") and to maintain it you must
> **continually interfere with voluntary exchanges that would otherwise upset it.**
> Your revocability requirement is a structural pattern, and liberty upsets it the
> moment someone freely chooses to bind themselves. To preserve your pattern you
> must forbid the free act. **T2 is not protection without paternalism; it is a
> pattern maintained by coercion — the very thing a side-constraint theory like
> yours and mine exists to forbid.**

The elegance of this kill is that it uses FDK's *own* anti-pattern, anti-aggregation
commitment against its *one* substantive addition. FDK cannot answer "patterns
require coercion to maintain" with "but welfare," because FDK already disowned
welfare. It is hoist by the side-constraint petard it shares with Nozick.

The two kills form a vise: **Kill A says FDK's historical silence under-protects
(victims of laundered theft); Kill B says FDK's one substantive rule over-reaches
(binding the self-binder).** Loosen one and the other tightens — adding a
rectification principle imports a pattern (Kill B grows); dropping revocability to
respect voluntary self-binding restores pure consent (FDK collapses to Rothbard,
Kill A's laundering remains).

## 3. Where the steelman is weakest — FDK's narrow counter-thrust

- **Against Kill B, FDK has a real reply — that revocability is a *side-constraint*,
  not a *pattern*.** This is the crux and it is genuinely arguable. Nozick's
  Chamberlain argument forbids *end-state and patterned* principles (about
  *distributions*); it explicitly *permits* historical and *side-constraint*
  principles. FDK can answer: "Revocability is not a pattern over outcomes; it is a
  constraint on the *form of a valid transfer* — exactly like your own rule that a
  transfer is just only if voluntary. You forbid transfers procured by force; I
  forbid transfers that foreclose their own reversibility. Both are constraints on
  *how* title moves, not patterns on *where* it ends up. Forbidding an
  exit-foreclosing transfer is no more 'pattern maintenance' than forbidding a
  coerced one." **If this holds, Kill B fails** — revocability earns side-constraint
  status and Chamberlain does not reach it. The unresolved hard question: is "the
  transferred title must remain revocable" a constraint on transfer-form (legitimate
  for Nozick) or a substantive restriction on *what may be alienated* (which needs
  the independent value Rothbard's prong B already demanded)? This is the same
  knife-edge as `01`'s §3 — and it is not settled here.
- **Against Kill A, FDK has *no* reply.** The rectification void is real and
  unpatched. FDK's only move is to concede and either (i) add a rectification
  principle — inheriting Nozick's unsolved specification problem *and* risking
  Kill B — or (ii) admit that on historical-injustice cases FDK is Nozick-minus, as
  `RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION.md` already conceded (O1: "≡ / ✗, Nozick-minus"). There is
  no third option that keeps the kernel's clean-chain audit and also stops the
  laundering.
- **The experience-machine point quietly helps Nozick, not FDK.** "What matters is
  actually living a life, not experiential states" is anti-welfarist *and*
  anti-purely-structural: it suggests legitimacy tracks the *real exercise of
  agency*, which is closer to Sen's capability-to-actually-do than to FDK's
  paper-availability of exit. FDK borrows the machine to beat utilitarians and does
  not notice it also indicts a structural test that counts an *unused, unwanted*
  exit as sufficient.

## 4. The death-condition, stated as a falsifier

> **If (A) FDK has no rectification principle and therefore issues ALLOW to holdings
> whose origin its own definition of title would condemn — laundering historical
> injustice — *and* (B) FDK's mandatory-revocability rule (T2) is a patterned
> restriction rather than a transfer-form side-constraint, so that maintaining it
> requires forbidding voluntary self-binding acts that liberty would otherwise
> produce — then FDK simultaneously under-protects (A) and over-reaches (B), and
> neither can be repaired without worsening the other. FDK is false: it is neither a
> complete entitlement theory (it lacks pillar 3) nor a consistent side-constraint
> theory (its one substantive rule is a coercively-maintained pattern).**

The two horns are not independent — that is the lethal part. The cheapest repair to
(A) (add rectification) is itself a pattern that sharpens (B); the cheapest repair to
(B) (drop revocability) re-exposes (A) by collapsing FDK into pure consent. The
falsifier is the **conjunction**, and the conjunction has no cheap escape.

## 5. Verdict — survive, partially survive, or die?

**Partially survives — and the surviving half is the one worth keeping.**

- **Kill A (rectification void): FDK takes the hit.** This is a genuine, conceded
  defeat on historical-injustice cases. FDK is Nozick-minus on O1: it lacks even his
  gesture at a third pillar, and its clean-chain ALLOW positively contradicts its own
  "legitimate origin" requirement. On this axis FDK does not merely tie Nozick — it
  is *incoherent against its own definition*, which is worse than redundant. **No
  survival here.**
- **Kill B (revocability as pattern): FDK has a live, possibly winning defense** —
  the side-constraint reclassification of §3. *If* revocability is a constraint on
  transfer-form (like the voluntariness constraint Nozick himself accepts), then
  Chamberlain never reaches it and Kill B fails. FDK does not *win* this here; it
  earns a stay of execution that turns entirely on a question it has not yet
  answered: is reversibility a *form* of valid transfer or a *substantive limit on
  alienability*? **Survives as an open defense, not a refutation.**

Net: FDK dies on rectification (Kill A) and survives — contingently — on revocability
(Kill B). Because the program's distinctive bet was never rectification (that node was
already conceded open), the *load-bearing* claim survives Nozick. But it survives only
by promising an argument it still owes.

## 6. On the central axis — REDUNDANT or DISTINCT?

**Distinct from Nozick — and that is exactly why it is in danger.**

Unlike Rothbard (where FDK's clean verdicts *are* the NAP, so the charge is
redundancy), FDK is genuinely **distinct** from Nozick on the load-bearing axis,
because T2 (mandatory revocability) is a constraint Nozick's entitlement theory does
*not* contain — Nozick would let a fully voluntary irrevocable transfer stand. So the
RIVAL "verdict-identity" worry does not bite here; FDK and Nozick *diverge* on the
voluntary-lifetime-binding case (FDK: DENY-to-enter; Nozick: ALLOW).

But distinctness is not vindication. The divergence is precisely the spot Kill B
attacks, and FDK's defense (it is a side-constraint, not a pattern) is *owed, not
paid*. So the honest finding inverts the Rothbard result:

- Against **Rothbard**, FDK's danger is **redundancy** — it mostly *is* him.
- Against **Nozick**, FDK's danger is **distinctness without justification** — it
  *differs* from him by adding exactly the constraint his Chamberlain argument is
  built to dissolve, and FDK has not yet shown the constraint is a legitimate
  side-constraint rather than a coercively-maintained pattern.

The two dossiers converge on one demand. Both Rothbard's prong B and Nozick's Kill B
ask the **same** question from opposite traditions: *is mandatory revocability a
structural condition on valid transfer, or a smuggled substantive value about open
futures?* If it is the former, FDK occupies its empty chair and survives both. If it
is the latter, Rothbard's "incoherent carve-out" and Nozick's "pattern that liberty
upsets" are the **same wound** seen twice — and FDK has neither protection nor
non-paternalism, only a preference it forgot to argue for. That question is not
answered by Rothbard or Nozick. It is answered, or not, at node K — by Sen.
