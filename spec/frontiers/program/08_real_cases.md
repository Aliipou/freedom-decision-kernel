# Phase 5 — Real cases and a falsifiable prediction

> Phases 1–4 sharpened definitions, wrote the falsifier, ran the rivals on toy
> rows, and laid the inference spine. None of that touches the world. This file
> applies FDK's exit-right reading to **real** historical and contemporary cases
> and then states one empirical claim that could come out against FDK. The
> question driving every case is narrow and unforgiving: **does the exit-right
> lens add anything beyond bare consent (Rothbard) or welfare/capability (Sen),
> or is it redescribing a verdict they already reach?** Three outcomes are
> admissible per case: FDK is *different-and-better*, *different-and-worse*, or
> *merely redescriptive*. We mark each.

The cases are grouped by **which open node they stress** (see
[`../EXIT_RIGHT_PROGRAM.md`](../EXIT_RIGHT_PROGRAM.md), nodes G and K), because
that grouping *is* the finding: FDK's candidate strength clusters in one column
and its threats cluster in the other two.

---

## Group A — Cases decided by a CLEAN exit-right (no node-G or node-K dependence)

Here exit is removed *structurally* — by the form of the arrangement, not by the
cost of leaving or by what the person has come to want. T2/T3/T4 fire directly.
This is FDK's candidate home.

### A1 — Chattel slavery (historical, e.g. antebellum US)

- **FDK reading.** The arrangement is a transfer of the person's body that
  *forecloses its own revocation*: the enslaved cannot withdraw and end future
  obligation. Even a sincerely-affirmed bond (the rare "loyal" case) is invalid,
  because revocability is constitutive of ownership and not the owner's to
  alienate (T2 → T3). DENY, and DENY independently of any welfare claim.
- **Considered judgment.** Match. Slavery is the paradigm illegitimacy.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Mostly redescriptive, but with one real
  edge.** Rothbard also denies most slavery (no valid title to a person), but is
  *indeterminate* on the voluntarily-affirmed lifetime bond — some libertarians
  permit it. FDK closes that gap structurally without reaching for the slave's
  welfare. Sen denies it via capability-destruction, i.e. *via* welfare. FDK's
  edge: it denies the *contented* slave's bond on the same ground as the
  resisting slave's — the alienated exit — not on how either feels. **Verdict:
  different-and-better than Rothbard on the affirmed-bond sub-case; tied with Sen
  on the verdict but cleaner on the ground.**

### A2 — At-will vs. bonded / indentured labor contracts

- **FDK reading.** At-will employment: legitimate — exit standing is preserved
  (either party ends future obligation). Indenture / bonded labor / contracts
  with criminal penalties for quitting (historical truck systems, some Gulf
  *kafala* arrangements): **DENY** — they remove or criminalize exit. The line is
  drawn by *whether quitting ends future obligation*, not by wage level or
  conditions.
- **Considered judgment.** Match, and notably it tracks where actual law evolved
  (abolition of penal sanctions for breach of labor contract).
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Different-and-better than Rothbard**, who
  treats a freely-signed bond term as binding; FDK voids the *non-exit* clause
  while leaving the rest of the contract standing — a surgical result. Sen
  reaches the same verdict but folds in working conditions, so Sen would also
  flag a *low-wage but exitable* job that FDK passes. That divergence is a
  feature for FDK (protection without paternalism) only if our considered
  judgment agrees the exitable low-wage job is *legitimate though bad* — which it
  arguably does. **Verdict: different-and-better; clean candidate.**

### A3 — Digital platform lock-in (switching cost, data non-portability, network effects)

- **FDK reading.** Split the case. Where lock-in is *engineered to foreclose
  exit* — non-portable data, deliberate interoperability sabotage, account
  deletion that destroys identity/history — the consent grounding continued use
  is invalid (manufactured-consent clause; the will to stay was produced by
  removing the alternative). DENY / remediate (mandate portability = restore
  exit). Where lock-in is mere *organic network effect* (everyone is there
  because everyone is there), no boundary was crossed without consent and FDK
  says ALLOW — option-poverty from others' legitimate prior choices is **not** a
  crossing (the [UNDERDETERMINED] clause of `Freedom` in Phase 1).
- **Considered judgment.** Match on the engineered cases (data portability law,
  e.g. GDPR Art. 20, encodes exactly this). On the organic-network-effect case,
  judgments split — and FDK's "ALLOW" is *contestable*, which is honest.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Different-and-better than both** on the
  engineered sub-case: Rothbard sees a voluntary user and says ALLOW; Sen reaches
  for the user's diminished capabilities. FDK pinpoints the wrong as *the removal
  of the revocation right itself*, which is why the remedy it implies (forced
  portability — restore exit) is more targeted than Sen's (which licenses
  open-ended welfare intervention) and unavailable to Rothbard. **Verdict:
  different-and-better; the strongest contemporary candidate.**

> **Group A is FDK's territory.** In all three, the verdict turns on whether the
> *form* of the arrangement preserves exit — decidable without reading welfare
> and without node G or K. This is where the empty chair ("protection without
> paternalism") is actually sat in.

## Group B — Cases that hinge on node G (exit EXISTS but is ruinous)

Exit is available on paper. The question is whether its *cost* defeats it. FDK
cannot answer these without closing node G — the exit-cost threshold — and any
threshold risks re-importing the welfare judgment FDK exists to avoid. **These
are not wins; they are exposures.**

### B1 — Consumer debt / payday-loan traps

- **FDK reading.** Borrowing is exitable in principle (repay, default,
  bankruptcy). But where the structure is engineered so that exit means ruin —
  rollover spirals, balloon terms, debt that survives bankruptcy (some student
  loan regimes) — FDK *wants* to say the exit is a fiction. It can only say so by
  pricing "ruin," which is node G. Without G, FDK's verdict is **schematic**: it
  agrees DENY *if* the cost crosses a threshold it has not defined.
- **Considered judgment.** Most people judge predatory debt traps illegitimate.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Redescriptive at best, and possibly worse.**
  To call ruinous-cost exit "no exit," FDK must specify *how ruinous* — and the
  moment it does ("exit that destroys the person's solvency/shelter/citizenship"),
  it has imported a welfare floor under a different name. Sen does this openly and
  more richly. Rothbard says ALLOW (a freely-signed loan). **Verdict: undecided,
  leaning redescriptive-of-Sen; node-G exposure.**

### B2 — Monopoly / "take it or leave it" and company towns

- **FDK reading.** The company town is the cleanest case in the column. Wages
  paid in scrip, housing tied to employment, the nearest free market a hundred
  miles off: exit *exists* (walk out) but costs you home, income, and community
  at once. FDK's instinct is DENY; its license to do so depends entirely on
  whether that bundled cost crosses node G.
- **Considered judgment.** Company towns are widely judged coercive — historical
  reform (Pullman, the truck acts) bears this out.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Borderline.** There is a *structural* reading
  available that keeps FDK clean: if the employer engineered the
  no-alternatives condition (built the town to capture, paid in non-exportable
  scrip), that is manufactured consent (Group A logic) and FDK denies it without
  pricing welfare. But if the isolation is merely *geographic bad luck* not
  produced by the employer, FDK has no crossing to point at and falls back to
  node G. **Verdict: different-and-better when the foreclosure is engineered;
  redescriptive-or-silent when it is ambient. The case splits on whose act
  produced the trap.**

> **Group B is where node G is load-bearing.** FDK's results here are *promissory*
> — valid only after the exit-cost threshold is defined, and at risk of becoming
> Sen-in-disguise the moment it is.

## Group C — Cases that hinge on node K (exit AVAILABLE but unwanted)

Exit stands and is affordable. The person *does not want it* — the preference has
adapted, or was authored upstream. This is the wall from Phase 2. FDK's
structural test says ALLOW (exit is real and unexercised by choice); our
considered judgment often says illegitimate. **This is where FDK is most likely
to lose to Sen.**

### C1 — Cults / high-control groups

- **FDK reading.** Two readings, and FDK must pick. (i) If the group *engineered
  the foreclosure* — sleep deprivation, information blockade, ties severed so
  leaving means total isolation — that is manufactured consent with a fictional
  exit, and FDK denies it on Group-A grounds *without* node K. (ii) If the member
  can in fact leave, is not blockaded, and simply *does not want to* because their
  preferences have been reshaped, FDK's structural test says **ALLOW** — and many
  would call that under-protection.
- **Considered judgment.** Split, and it tracks reading (i) vs. (ii): we condemn
  the blockading cult; we are genuinely unsure about the contented, free-to-leave
  devotee.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Different-and-better on (i)** (evidence of
  engineered foreclosure converts an authenticity question into a structural
  one — FDK's distinctive move), but **different-and-worse on (ii)**, where Sen's
  capability/adaptive-preference reading catches a wrong FDK passes. The case is
  decided by *evidence of engineering*, which is exactly the Phase 1 flag on
  brainwashing ("DENY *if* engineered foreclosure shown").

### C2 — Addiction (substances, gambling, engineered apps)

- **FDK reading.** The sharpest node-K case. The addict *can* quit (exit exists)
  and frequently *does not want to* (preference captured). FDK's structural test,
  read literally, says ALLOW — the exit is unexercised by the person's own will.
  FDK's only escape is to argue the *will itself was engineered* (dopamine-loop
  design, variable-ratio reinforcement in slot machines and feed apps), pushing
  the case back into manufactured consent (Group A). That works against the
  *designer* (the casino, the app) but **not** against substance addiction with
  no designer, which is pure node K.
- **Considered judgment.** We judge engineered-addiction products illegitimate
  (the designer crossed a boundary); we judge the addict's own continued use a
  tragedy but contest whether anyone's *legitimacy* was violated absent a
  designer.
- **Does the lens add anything?** **Different-and-better against the engineer**
  (locates the wrong in the capture-by-design, not in the user's welfare —
  licensing a targeted remedy: ban the dark pattern, not the user's choice).
  **Different-and-worse, or silent, on designerless addiction**, where Sen's
  adaptive-preference reading speaks and FDK's structural test does not. **This is
  node K in its purest form and FDK's clearest loss.**

> **Group C is where node K is load-bearing.** FDK survives only by *converting*
> the case into a Group-A foreclosure (find the engineer). Where no engineer
> exists — internalised preference with a real, open exit — FDK is silent or
> permissive, and Sen out-protects it. Phase 2 named this; the real cases confirm
> it is not hypothetical.

## The honest scorecard

| Case | Node | FDK verdict | vs. Rothbard | vs. Sen | Outcome for FDK |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chattel slavery | clean | DENY | better on affirmed bond | cleaner ground | different-and-better (narrow) |
| Bonded vs. at-will labor | clean | DENY non-exit clause | better | surgical, less paternalist | **different-and-better** |
| Platform lock-in (engineered) | clean | DENY/remediate | better | more targeted remedy | **different-and-better** |
| Consumer debt trap | G | DENY *if* cost > G | better-or-tied | redescriptive | undecided → exposure |
| Company town / monopoly | G | DENY if engineered | better if engineered | silent if ambient | splits |
| Cult (blockading) | A via C | DENY | better | tied | different-and-better |
| Cult (free-to-leave devotee) | K | ALLOW | tied | **worse** | loss |
| Addiction (engineered app/casino) | A via C | DENY designer | better | targeted | different-and-better |
| Addiction (designerless) | K | ALLOW | tied | **worse** | **loss** |

**Count.** Of nine real-case readings, FDK is plausibly *different-and-better*
on **four** (bonded labor, engineered platform lock-in, blockading cult,
engineered-addiction products) — all of which are, on inspection, **the same
structural move**: a boundary was crossed by *engineering the foreclosure of
exit*, and FDK names that wrong without reading welfare. It is *redescriptive or
exposed* on the node-G cases (debt, ambient monopoly), and it **loses** on the
two pure node-K cases (free-to-leave devotee, designerless addiction).

The single best candidate is **engineered platform lock-in** (A3): contemporary,
legally live (data-portability law already encodes the exit-restoration remedy),
and the place where FDK's verdict and remedy are *both* unavailable to Rothbard
and *more targeted* than Sen's. The case that most threatens the thesis is
**designerless addiction** (C2): a real, affordable, open exit that the person
does not want, with no manipulator to convert it into a Group-A foreclosure —
node K with nowhere to hide.

## The prediction (this is the point of Phase 5)

Retrodiction above is necessary but not sufficient; the thesis must risk a
*forward* claim about the world.

> **Claim.** Across arrangements **matched on stated satisfaction**, the
> exit-poor ones (company towns, debt-bondage regimes, high-lock-in platforms)
> exhibit measurably **lower realised mobility and higher coerced-stay** —
> longer tenure not explained by satisfaction, lower account/app deletion under
> equal expressed approval, lower out-migration at equal reported contentment —
> than exit-rich ones. **And it is the *exit gap*, not the satisfaction level,
> that tracks the considered legitimacy judgment.**

**Data domains.**
- *Labor mobility:* job-to-job transition rates and tenure in bonded/penal-breach
  vs. at-will regimes, conditioned on reported job satisfaction.
- *Company towns:* out-migration rates from scrip/tied-housing towns vs.
  comparable free-labor towns at matched reported contentment.
- *Platforms:* account-deletion and app-uninstall rates under dark-pattern vs.
  clean (portable, low-friction) designs, conditioned on satisfaction scores.
- *Debt:* refinance/exit and bankruptcy-utilisation rates in predatory vs.
  conventional credit, at matched borrower-reported acceptance.

**What would REFUTE it.** If, holding stated satisfaction equal, exit-poor and
exit-rich arrangements are **behaviorally indistinguishable** — same realised
mobility, same deletion/uninstall, same out-migration — then *exit has no
empirical signature distinct from satisfaction*, the "structural exit"
distinction collapses into preference, and **FDK is chasing a phantom**: every
Group-A verdict it claimed as distinctive could be recovered from satisfaction
data alone, and Sen's welfare reading was sufficient all along.

A **partial** refutation is equally informative and more likely: if the exit gap
shows up on the **engineered** cases (Group A) but *vanishes* on the node-G and
node-K cases at matched satisfaction, that would empirically confirm the
scorecard above — FDK's signature is real but **confined to engineered
foreclosure**, and absent where exit-cost (G) or adapted preference (K) is doing
the work. That outcome would not kill FDK; it would *bound* it, which is the
honest best case the program currently expects.

## Closing — different-and-better, or redescription?

On the count above, FDK's exit-right reading is genuinely *different-and-better*
than both Rothbard and Sen on roughly **four of nine** real cases, and those four
collapse to a **single mechanism**: the wrong of *engineering the foreclosure of
exit*, named structurally, without reading welfare. That is a real, narrow,
defensible territory — the empty chair is occupiable, but it is a small chair.

On the other five, FDK is either redescribing Sen (the node-G debt and ambient-
monopoly cases, where any non-paternalist exit-cost threshold remains undefined)
or losing to Sen outright (the node-K cases of free-to-leave devotion and
designerless addiction, where exit is real, open, and unwanted). The best single
candidate for FDK's contribution is **engineered platform lock-in**; the case
that most threatens the whole thesis is **designerless addiction**, where node K
admits no engineer to blame and the structural test simply says ALLOW where our
judgment says otherwise.

The verdict of Phase 5, stated plainly: **FDK is distinctive and right on
engineered-foreclosure cases, promissory on exit-cost cases pending node G, and
defeated on pure adaptive-preference cases pending node K.** Whether that
territory is large enough to call FDK a separate theory — rather than "Rothbard +
a structural exit clause that happens to agree with Sen where they overlap" — is
exactly what the prediction above is built to decide.
