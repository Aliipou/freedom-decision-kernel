# How Rothbard kills FDK — the redundancy execution

> Phase 2 of the Exit-Right Program. This file does not defend FDK against
> Rothbard; it builds the **strongest Rothbardian** and lets him swing. The thesis
> under test is FDK's only candidate ✚ from `RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION.md`: that a
> *mandatory-revocability* condition on consent gives FDK a distinctive,
> non-paternalist territory. Rothbard's reply is that this condition is the one
> place FDK stops being consent-theory and starts importing a value — and that
> everywhere else, FDK is his system with a proof assistant bolted on. Three
> outcomes are admissible below: FDK survives, partially survives, or dies.

## 1. The strongest Rothbard (steelman, his actual commitments)

Rothbard's system is not a mood; it is a derivation, and a tight one. Stated at
full strength, on his own terms:

- **Self-ownership as the non-negotiable axiom.** Each person has full property in
  their own body. Its alternatives are exactly two — partial ownership of everyone
  by everyone (incoherent, no one may act) or ownership of some by others
  (slavery) — and both are self-refuting for anyone arguing. This is the
  *argumentation* move (sharpened later by Hoppe): to dispute self-ownership you
  must exercise the very self-control you deny. FDK's "Person owns itself" is
  **Rothbard's axiom, restated**. FDK did not derive it; it imported it.
- **Original appropriation (homesteading).** Unowned nature becomes owned by the
  first to mix labor with it / bring it into use. This answers Ownership-Genesis
  for him; FDK leaves that node **[UNDERDETERMINED]** (O2) and so is, on genesis,
  *strictly weaker* than the man it wants to beat.
- **Title-transfer theory of contract.** This is the part FDK readers
  underestimate. For Rothbard a contract is **not** a binding promise; it is a
  *transfer of alienable titles*. You can only contract over what you can alienate.
  This single move already does much of the work FDK thinks revocability does —
  see §3.
- **Non-aggression.** The only illegitimate act is initiated force/fraud against
  person or property. Note this is **boundary-crossing without consent** — which
  is, line for line, FDK's `Legitimate(action) ⟺ every boundary crossed has the
  valid consent of its owner`. FDK's core *is* the non-aggression principle
  expressed in a typed predicate.
- **Inalienability, stated precisely (the nuance the task demands).** Rothbard does
  **not** say "voluntary slavery is fine." His actual position: the *will* is
  inalienable because you cannot in fact transfer control of your own body's future
  acts — control remains physically yours. Therefore a "slavery contract" transfers
  *no enforceable title to your person*; you may walk away. What you *can* be held
  to is **transferred alienable property**: if you took $10,000 on a promise to
  serve and then quit, you owe back the *property* (the money/title), not your body.
  So Rothbard reaches a verdict adjacent to FDK's — the slave can leave — but from
  **alienability of titles**, not from a posited "exit right."
- **Children — his admitted soft spot, stated honestly.** Rothbard holds a parent
  has no obligation to feed a child (only a duty not to aggress), and a child
  acquires self-ownership by "running away" / demonstrating self-control. He has
  **no competence threshold** and notoriously cannot say *when* a child may bind
  itself. FDK's `competent` flag fills exactly this hole — but, per S1, the flag is
  a black box FDK imports, not a thing it derives. So FDK is determinate where
  Rothbard is vague *only by assuming the answer*.

This is a man with one axiom, one acquisition rule, one contract theory, and one
prohibition, from which every clean libertarian verdict falls out. He is the
parsimony champion. That matters, because FDK's *only* refuge from the redundancy
charge (per the RIVAL refinement) is "a more unifying derivation" — and parsimony
is the field Rothbard already owns.

## 2. The sharpest attack — the REDUNDANCY kill

The kill has two prongs. The first guts FDK's clean cases; the second turns its one
original move into a self-inflicted wound.

**Prong A — "Every clean verdict is mine."** Walk the determinate cases of
`00_definitions.md` and `RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION.md`:

| Case | FDK verdict | Rothbard verdict | Who derived it first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boundary crossing w/o consent | DENY | aggression ⇒ DENY | Rothbard (NAP) |
| Homesteading unowned resource (O2) | ALLOW | ALLOW (first use) | Rothbard/Locke |
| Clean-chain title from old conquest (O1) | ALLOW today's holder | ALLOW (no identifiable victim) | Rothbard |
| Taxation for public good (A2) | DENY | DENY (theft) | Rothbard |
| 12-yo organ sale (S1) | DENY (incompetent) | DENY-ish (no theory) | tie, FDK by *imported* flag |
| Commons binds dissenter (A1) | DENY | DENY (aggression on the dissenter) | Rothbard |

Rothbard's claim is not "we agree on these." It is stronger: **FDK reaches them by
re-deriving the NAP.** `Legitimate ⟺ consent-of-every-boundary-owner` is the
non-aggression principle with the quantifier made explicit. The typed kernel, the
ALLOW/DENY/DEFER trichotomy, the proof obligations — these are *engineering of
Rothbard's ethics*, not a rival ethics. FDK itself half-concedes this: its stated
fallback outcome is "Rothbard + formal methods."

**Prong B — the one new move is where you abandon consent-purism.** FDK's sole ✚ is
T2: *consent is valid only if revocable; non-revocable consent alienates the exit
right, which is not the owner's to alienate.* Rothbard's reply is lethal precisely
because he is **also an inalienability theorist** and can therefore meet FDK on its
own ground rather than from outside:

1. *You and I agree the contract is unenforceable against the body — but for
   opposite reasons, and yours is the incoherent one.* My reason: the will cannot
   *in fact* be transferred, so no title to it ever moves; the slave walks because
   there was never anything for the master to hold. **A fact about ownership.** Your
   reason: there is a free-standing "exit right" that is "not the owner's to
   alienate." **A restriction on ownership.** You have told me the owner does *not*
   own one of their own rights. By your own settled ruling Ownership is BINARY and
   self-ownership is total — so *which* sub-right did the self-owner mysteriously
   fail to receive title to, and from whom? You have smuggled a **carve-out of
   self-ownership** and called it a feature.
2. *That carve-out is a value I reject, not a structural fact.* "A person may not
   bind their own future irrevocably" is a **paternalist premise about what is good
   for selves** — the exact welfare-adjacent judgment FDK swears off. You did not
   avoid Sen; you became a Sen with one fixed preference (for open futures) hard-
   coded, and no argument for it but assertion.
3. *Title-transfer already does your protective work without the carve-out.* The
   locked-in worker, the company-town resident, the debt-bonded — I free every one
   of them the moment I ask "what *alienable title* did they actually transfer?" If
   the answer is "their future labor / their body / their right to leave," I say:
   **those are not alienable titles, so no transfer occurred, so they may walk and
   owe only returned property.** I reach FDK's protective verdict (C2) from
   alienability, claiming **no new principle**. So even where FDK looks distinctive,
   the work is done by a Rothbardian primitive FDK duplicated under a new name.

The combined charge: *Where FDK is clean it is the NAP. Where FDK is original
(T2) it is either (i) my own inalienability point relabeled — redundant — or (ii) a
genuinely new restriction on self-ownership — in which case it is an imported
anti-future-binding value, and FDK's "no welfare, no paternalism" boast is false.
You are either me, or you are incoherent.*

## 3. Where the steelman is weakest — FDK's narrow counter-thrust

Honesty requires stating Rothbard's exposed flank, because FDK's survival, if any,
lives only here.

- **Title-transfer does *not* clearly catch the contented-future case.** Rothbard
  frees the slave who *wants out* (he walks; nothing held him). But the case that
  matters — *pre-committing to remove your own future exit and meaning it* — is
  where the two theories may finally part. Rothbard: you owe back any property
  advanced, and otherwise you are free, full stop; if you choose to stay, that is
  your self-ownership in action. FDK: the *arrangement itself*, in foreclosing
  revocation, is illegitimate to enter, independent of property owed. This is a real
  divergence — but note its direction: FDK is the one adding a constraint Rothbard's
  consent-purism omits. That makes FDK **distinct**, not obviously **right** (the
  rightness is litigated at node K, the contented slave).
- **Children.** Rothbard's "runs away" criterion is genuinely worse than FDK's
  binary-ownership / graded-authority split, which can say "owns body, lacks
  authority-to-transfer" without ever fractioning title. *If* FDK's competence
  threshold can be **derived** rather than imported (S1's open challenge), FDK is
  more determinate here without welfare. That derivation does not yet exist. Until
  it does, the advantage is promissory.
- **Genesis.** No counter. Rothbard answers Ownership-Genesis (homesteading); FDK
  flags it open. Strict loss for FDK on parsimony's home turf.

## 4. The death-condition, stated as a falsifier

> **If FDK's mandatory-revocability condition (T2) is either (a) extensionally
> equivalent to Rothbard's alienability-of-titles theory — reaching every verdict
> his title-transfer theory already reaches — or (b) a genuine *additional*
> restriction on self-ownership that cannot be justified except by an imported
> value (open futures / anti-irrevocable-binding) that FDK's own anti-paternalism
> charter forbids — then FDK is, on this axis, either redundant with Rothbard or
> incoherent by its own lights, and has no distinctive *and* defensible territory.
> FDK is false as a distinctive theory.**

The disjunction is the trap: **(a) is the redundancy horn, (b) is the incoherence
horn.** FDK escapes only by finding a case where T2 (i) gives a *different* verdict
from title-transfer *and* (ii) that difference is defensible **without** appeal to a
welfare/flourishing/open-future value. The whole program is the search for that one
case.

## 5. Verdict — survive, partially survive, or die?

**Partially survives — barely, and not where it hoped.**

- On the clean cases (S1, O1, O2, A2, A1, core NAP): **dies into Rothbard.** These
  are the non-aggression principle re-derived. FDK adds rigor, types, and a decision
  procedure — real engineering value, *zero* theoretical distinction. Verdict-
  identity here is near-total, and Rothbard's single-axiom derivation matches or
  beats FDK's on parsimony, so even the RIVAL "more unifying derivation" escape is
  contested, not won.
- On genesis: **loses.** Rothbard answers; FDK flags open.
- On the one ✚ (revocability / lock-in, C2): **survives only as a live question.**
  Against the *exit-wanting* victim, title-transfer already liberates them, so FDK
  is redundant there too. FDK becomes genuinely distinct **only** in the
  *exit-foreclosing-but-sincerely-chosen* case — and there it is distinct by
  *adding a constraint Rothbard refuses*, which throws the whole weight onto whether
  that constraint is defensible (node K) rather than paternalist (Rothbard's prong
  B-2). FDK cannot answer that here; it punts to `03_how_sen_kills_fdk.md`.

## 6. On the central axis — REDUNDANT or DISTINCT?

**Mostly redundant; distinct on a knife-edge that Sen, not Rothbard, will test.**

The precise finding: FDK is **redundant** with Rothbard wherever the victim would
*take* an exit if offered — because Rothbard's title-transfer theory already offers
it (no alienable title to the body ever moved, so the door was always open). FDK is
**distinct** from Rothbard at exactly one point: it declares the exit-foreclosing
*arrangement* illegitimate to **enter**, not merely unenforceable to **hold** — a
constraint Rothbard's consent-purism rejects as itself a violation of self-ownership.

So the redundancy kill **succeeds against most of FDK and reframes the rest**: FDK's
sole surviving claim to distinctiveness is not a new verdict but a new *constraint*,
and Rothbard has already named the price of that constraint — it is a carve-out of
self-ownership that needs a justifying value FDK has not produced. Whether that value
is paternalism (Rothbard wins) or a defensible structural condition (FDK's empty
chair is real) is **not decided here**. Rothbard pushes FDK off every square except
one, and on that last square hands it to Sen to finish. The honest report: against
Rothbard, FDK is *Rothbard-plus-one-contested-constraint* — and the constraint's
defense is owed, not paid.
