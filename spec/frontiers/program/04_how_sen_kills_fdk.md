# How Sen Kills FDK

> Phase 2 of the Exit-Right Program — **node K, the central kill.** This is the wall
> the entire program was built to be thrown against. The other dossiers bound FDK's
> scope; this one tests whether FDK has a distinctive territory *at all*. The brief
> is adversarial and honest: steelman Sen at full strength, then locate the exact
> case where FDK breaks, and admit which of the three outcomes (survive / partial /
> collapse) the argument actually delivers. The honest finding, stated up front so
> the reader can audit it against the argument: **partial collapse, bordering on
> full collapse on the pure case.** The exit-right move buys real ground against
> *manufactured* consent and *no* ground against *adaptive* consent — and adaptive
> consent is the case the program named as its own falsifier.

---

## 1. Sen at full strength (no strawman)

Sen is not a utilitarian and not a Rawlsian, and FDK must not file him under
either. His commitments, faithfully:

- **The capability approach.** What matters for freedom is not resources held nor
  utility felt but **capabilities**: the real, effective opportunities a person has
  to achieve the *functionings* (being nourished, literate, able to appear in
  public without shame, to participate) she has reason to value. Freedom is the
  *size and quality of the option set actually open to her*, not what she ends up
  choosing or feeling.
- **Functionings vs. capabilities.** A functioning is an achieved state; a
  capability is the freedom to achieve it. Two people with the same functioning
  (both fasting) differ in capability (one starving, one choosing to fast) — and
  the difference *is* the freedom. Sen's freedom is **agency over a real feasible
  set**, not the contentment of the chosen state.
- **The critique of utility.** Utility (pleasure, desire-satisfaction, stated
  preference) is a corrupted metric for well-being and freedom, because **desires
  adapt to circumstance.** "A grumbling rich man may well be less happy than a
  contented peasant, but he does have a higher standard of living." The
  thoroughly deprived — the landless laborer, the subjugated woman — learn to take
  pleasure in small mercies and to *not want* what they have no hope of getting.
  Their satisfied preference *registers their deprivation as contentment*. To
  measure freedom by satisfied preference is to count the success of the
  oppression as the freedom of the oppressed.
- **The critique of primary goods (the strike on Rawls, relevant here).** Even
  Rawls's resourcist metric fails, because persons differ in their *conversion* of
  goods into capabilities (the disabled, the ill, the pregnant need more to reach
  the same functioning). Freedom must be read at the level of *real effective
  options*, not goods and not, emphatically, not satisfaction.
- **Adaptive preference — the load-bearing case.** The contented housewife who has
  internalized that her interests rank below her husband's and *sincerely* reports
  no grievance; the chronically ill in deprived regions who, in Sen's Indian
  health surveys, report *less* morbidity than the better-off and better-informed,
  because they have never known health and do not perceive their condition as
  illness; the uncomplaining poor. Their consent — their stated preference, their
  "yes," their reported contentment — is **sincere, unmanipulated, and exactly as
  deformed as their circumstances.** No fraud, no coercer, no manipulator, no
  removed exit. The preference itself adapted, over a lifetime, to the feasible set.
- **Agency vs. well-being.** Sen distinguishes a person's *agency* (her capacity to
  pursue goals she has reason to value) from her *well-being*. He locates freedom in
  the **capability set**, so he can call the adaptive preferrer *unfree* — her
  option set is foreclosed — *without* merely overriding her stated wish: he is not
  telling her what to want, he is observing what she cannot reach.

This last point is the steelman FDK most needs to feel: Sen's verdict on the
contented slave does **not** reduce to crude "we know your interests better than
you." It is a verdict about the *structure of her options*, which Sen reads
directly. That is precisely the move FDK claimed for itself — and the question of
this file is whether FDK can make it without becoming Sen.

## 2. The kill (node K)

Set the case at its purest, stripped of every feature FDK *can* catch, so that only
the naked phenomenon remains. This is `consent_authenticity` §2 case #32 and
RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION C1, at maximum strength:

> A subordinated person — call her the **contented slave** — sincerely consents to
> her subordination. Construct the case so that:
> - her consent is **informed, specific, competent, voluntary** — all true, not
>   attested-true-but-false; she understands her situation completely;
> - there is **no manipulator**, no fraud, no deceiver, no coercer; no party
>   crossed a boundary to produce the preference;
> - the preference adapted over a lifetime, *upstream* of any act the gate can see;
> - and — the decisive stipulation — **a real exit exists.** She *can* leave, at a
>   cost that does not destroy her. The door is unlocked. She has the key, knows it,
>   and **does not want to use it.** Her preference has adapted so that the exit,
>   though real, is *unwanted*.

Now run FDK. The exit-right condition (T2: consent valid only if revocable) is
FDK's whole distinctive weapon. **It does not fire**, because by stipulation the
exit *is* preserved — the arrangement does not remove it, the consent *is*
revocable, she simply declines to revoke. Every clause of validity holds. The gate
returns **ALLOW**. FDK certifies the contented slave's subordination as legitimate.

Sen returns **DENY** (her capability set is foreclosed; she is unfree regardless of
her contentment), and Sen is, here, on the side of nearly every considered moral
judgment. This is the divergence. The question is whether FDK's divergence is
*distinctive-and-right* or *distinctive-and-wrong*. The argument below is that it
is wrong, and that the wrongness is **forced** — there is no repair inside FDK's
architecture.

### Why FDK's best move does not reach the case

FDK's strongest available defense is **"exit must be *real*, not nominal."** This is
the genuine content of node G: a door that opens onto ruin, statelessness,
starvation, or death is no door; an exit so costly it would destroy the person does
not count, so FDK can DENY the company town, the debt-bondage trap, the
roach-motel, the lock-in — every case where the exit is *fictional*. That move is
real and it is FDK's best ground (it is the entire force of the C2 ✚ in
RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION, and the whole of Kill A's partial survival against Rawls).

**But the contented-slave case is constructed precisely past that move.** The exit
is stipulated *real* — affordable, survivable, known. The only thing wrong with it
is that she does not *want* it. And "she does not want the real exit" is exactly
the input FDK is forbidden, by its own identity, to second-guess:

- FDK reads **consent, not preference-correctness** (`Consent ≠ Preference`, but
  the gate's only handle on the will is the sincere "yes," which here is present
  and valid).
- FDK reads **exit-right, not exit-taken** (`Exit-right ≠ Exit-taken`) — but this
  cuts *against* FDK here, not for it: the principle says "she stayed" does not
  prove consent, yet in this case she didn't merely stay, she *sincerely affirms*,
  and the exit-right is genuinely present. The separation that protects the
  locked-in (don't infer consent from staying) gives FDK *nothing* to deny the
  contented slave (whose consent is independently valid).
- FDK **never reads welfare, capability, or interest.** That refusal is its anti-
  paternalist spine and its claim to the empty chair.

So the wall: to deny the contented slave, FDK must find the arrangement illegitimate
*despite* valid consent and *despite* a real preserved exit. The **only** remaining
ground for a DENY is a judgment that her *adapted preference is itself defective* —
that her sincere will does not track her freedom. And the moment FDK asserts that,
it has imported exactly Sen's interest-judgment: it is reading the *capability set*
behind the preference and overriding the stated will against it. That is the
horn-B/paternalism move FDK exists to refuse. There is no structural fact left to
read, because **the distortion is not in any structure the gate can see — it is in
the preference itself, formed upstream, over a lifetime, by no nameable act.**

This is why node K is *the* wall and not one objection among many. Every *other*
manipulated-consent case (the §2 catalogue of consent_authenticity — dark patterns,
dopamine loops, cults, payday traps) has a **structural residue** FDK can in
principle reach: a manipulator who crossed a boundary, an engineered foreclosure of
the exit, a removed alternative, a fraud, a roach-motel asymmetry that makes the
*real* exit cost detectable. For those, the exit-right + authenticity machinery has
*something to grip*. The contented slave is the **one case with zero structural
residue**: real exit, no manipulator, sincere informed valid consent, distortion
located entirely inside a preference the theory is sworn not to audit. The exit-
right program cannot be built to catch it, because it is defined by the absence of
everything the program detects.

## 3. The death condition, stated as a falsifier

> **Death condition K.** *If* there exists a person whose consent to her own
> subordination is sincere, informed, specific, competent, and voluntary; whose
> arrangement preserves a **real** (affordable, survivable) exit she simply does not
> want; with **no** manipulator, fraud, coercion, or removed alternative — *and* our
> considered moral judgment is that her subordination is **illegitimate** (the
> contented slave is *wronged*, not merely unfortunate) — *then* a purely structural
> exit-right test issues the **wrong** verdict (ALLOW), and FDK can correct it only
> by reading the deformation of her preference, which is Sen's capability/interest
> judgment. **If that case is real and that judgment is correct, FDK is false** as
> "protection without paternalism": on the adaptive-preference case it either
> **under-protects** (ALLOW = Rothbard) or **imports the interest-judgment** (DENY =
> Sen). There is no third verdict its architecture permits.

The falsifier is tight and the program wrote it itself (EXIT_RIGHT_PROGRAM Phase 2,
node K). What this dossier adds is the demonstration that FDK's *best* counter — "the
exit must be real" — does not touch the falsifier, because the falsifier is
constructed with the exit *already* real and the defect relocated into the
unwanting. The exit-realness move answers G, not K. **G and K are different walls,
and clearing G does not clear K.** This is the load-bearing finding of the file.

## 4. Honest verdict: PARTIAL COLLAPSE, bordering on full collapse

The three outcomes, adjudicated against the argument, not the hope.

**Where FDK survives.** Against *manufactured* consent with a *foreclosed or fake*
exit — the company town, debt bondage, lifetime-slavery contracts, platform
lock-in, the roach motel — FDK's exit-right condition does real, distinctive,
non-welfarist work. It DENIES these structurally, where Rothbard waves them through
on bare consent and where Sen can only DENY by importing capability. That is a
genuine territory and it is not Sen's: FDK reaches the *would-leave-if-they-could*
victim by reading the exit, not the welfare. The empty chair is **occupiable for
the manufactured-but-not-yet-internalised case.** This survival is real and should
not be discounted to make the collapse cleaner.

**Where FDK partially collapses.** The territory it occupies is **strictly smaller
than it claimed.** FDK's pitch was protection of *the locked-in and the
manufactured-into-consent* "like Sen/Rawls do, but without their welfare judgment."
The contented slave is the limit of "manufactured-into-consent," and FDK does *not*
reach her. So the distinctive region shrinks to its honest boundary: **consent that
is manufactured *and not yet fully internalised* — where a structural residue (a
foreclosed exit, a live manipulator, a removed alternative) still exists to grip.**
The instant internalisation is complete and the exit is real, FDK's grip is gone.
The chair is occupiable, but it is a smaller chair than advertised, and Sen's chair
contains it.

**Where FDK borders on full collapse.** On the *pure* adaptive-preference case —
real exit, complete internalisation, zero residue — FDK does not partially fail; it
returns the **flatly wrong verdict** (ALLOW), and its only correction *is* becoming
Sen. RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION already graded this case **⊥ (collapse, FDK worse than a
rival)** and **✗ (distinctive-and-refuted)**, and this dossier confirms the grade
under the strongest construction: it is not that FDK is silent (as with Rawls's
non-identity), it is that FDK *speaks and is wrong*, legitimising a subordination
that nearly all considered judgment condemns. Within the pure case, this is full
collapse. The reason it is reported as *partial* collapse overall is only that the
pure case is a limit — most real subordination retains *some* structural residue,
so FDK's domain is non-empty. But the limit is exactly the case Sen built his
theory on, and it is exactly the case the exit-right program named as the
experiment that decides it. FDK loses that experiment.

**The decisive sentence.** Exit-right protects the person who *would leave if they
could*; it has nothing to say to the person who *could leave and does not want to*.
Sen's whole point is that the second person may be the more deeply unfree — her very
wanting has been foreclosed — and FDK's architecture is constitutionally unable to
see foreclosed wanting, because foreclosed wanting leaves no boundary uncrossed.
That is not a bug to be patched; it is the shadow cast by FDK's anti-paternalist
commitment. The same refusal that makes FDK *not Sen* on the manufactured case
makes FDK *wrong* on the adaptive case. You cannot keep the spine and reach the
contented slave.

## 5. Redundant or distinct on this axis?

On the adaptive-preference axis: **FDK is distinct-and-refuted, and on the pure case
collapses *toward* Sen.** It is not redundant with Sen — it gives the *opposite*
verdict (ALLOW vs. DENY) — but "distinct because wrong" is the worst kind of
distinctness. The only way FDK converts that wrong verdict to a right one is to
adopt Sen's reading of the option set behind the preference, at which point it *is*
Sen on this case and has surrendered "without paternalism." So the axis sorts FDK
into a forced dilemma with no distinctive-and-right cell:

| FDK's move on the contented slave | What FDK becomes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| ALLOW (honor the sincere, valid consent + real exit) | **Rothbard** | under-protects; refuted by considered judgment |
| DENY (the adapted preference doesn't track her freedom) | **Sen** | imports the interest-judgment; loses "without paternalism" |
| DEFER / advisory-only (flag, never override) | **honest, but not a verdict** | relocates the dilemma; the gate still ALLOWs |

The third row is FDK's actual current posture (the `fdk_research` advisory layer,
REQUEST_REVIEW). It is the *honest* move and it is worth keeping — but it is not a
solution to node K; it is the admission that the gate has none. The gate still
returns ALLOW; the advisory layer merely says "a human should look." That makes
FDK's *honesty* distinctive. It does not make FDK's *verdict* distinctive-and-right,
which was the bar.

**The bounded survivor.** The fairest statement that the argument supports: the
exit-right program is **distinct and defensible on the manufactured-consent /
real-lock-in region, and collapses on the fully-internalised adaptive-preference
region.** Its true home is therefore narrower than "the empty chair between Rothbard
and Sen" — it is the *sub-chair* of "manufactured consent with a structural residue
the gate can grip, before internalisation completes." That sub-chair is real, it is
not Rothbard's and not Sen's, and defending it rigorously is a genuine contribution.
But it is bought at the price of conceding the headline case. **The contented slave
stays in Sen's room. FDK does not get her back without becoming Sen — and the
honest report of node K is that this is the wall the program does not clear.**
