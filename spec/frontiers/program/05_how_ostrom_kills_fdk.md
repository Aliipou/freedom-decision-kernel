# Phase 2 — How Ostrom kills FDK (the commons falsifier)

> The Sen kill (`03`) attacks the consent axis from inside the person: the
> contented slave who does not *want* the exit. The Ostrom kill attacks it from
> the outside, on a different terrain — the **commons** — and it is, if anything,
> the harder of the two, because here FDK is not silent and not merely
> under-protective. Here FDK issues a *positive verdict* and a Nobel laureate
> spent forty years showing that verdict is empirically false. This file states
> that attack at full strength and writes its death-condition first.

This is the program-level companion to the hostile [`../aggregation.md`](../aggregation.md),
which catalogues 27 cases. This file does one thing instead: it turns that
catalogue into a single falsifier with a stated death-condition, so the exit-right
program can be killed cleanly on the commons rather than dying by a thousand cases.

## 0. What FDK says about a commons (derived, not asserted)

FDK's core primitive:

> `Legitimate(action) ⟺ ∀ boundary b crossed by action: valid_consent(owner(b), action)`

For a good that **no single person owns** — an alpine pasture, a river's flow, an
inshore fishery, an aquifer, the atmosphere — the quantifier `∀ b ∈ owned-boundaries`
ranges over the **empty set**. A universal over the empty set is *vacuously true*.
Two consequences follow by pure logic, with no welfare term anywhere:

1. **Every extraction is ALLOW.** Emptying an ownerless fishery crosses no owned
   boundary ⇒ vacuously legitimate ⇒ ALLOW. FDK licenses the race-to-the-bottom.
2. **Every conservation cap is DENY.** A cap binding a non-consenting commoner
   crosses *that commoner's* boundary (their person, their labour, their catch)
   without their consent ⇒ `coerces`/`confiscates` ⇒ DENY.

Put (1) and (2) together and FDK does not merely *fail* to govern a commons. It
**structurally derives the tragedy of the commons as a theorem**: unlimited
individual extraction is legitimate, and the only restraint that would prevent
collapse is illegitimate. Hardin (1968) — "Freedom in a commons brings ruin to
all" — is not cited by FDK, but it is the literal output of FDK's gate.

This is the crucial difference from the Sen kill. Against Sen, FDK is accused of
*under-protecting* (a true thing it cannot see). Against Ostrom, FDK is accused of
*mis-deciding* — emitting a determinate ALLOW/DENY pair that is wrong.

## 1. The empirical fact FDK collides with

Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* (1990) and the Workshop's thousands of catalogued
cases (the IFRI/NIIS corpora) establish, as well as anything in the field is
established:

- Real appropriators in real common-pool resources **frequently do not** deplete
  them. The tragedy is **contingent, not structural**.
- They sustain them for **centuries** — and these are not folklore:
  - **Törbel** (Swiss alpine commons): a village charter dated **1517** still
    governs the high alp by the *wintering rule* — no household may summer more
    cows than it can winter — meadows held privately, the alp held in common,
    ~700 years without depletion or privatization.
  - **Valencia huerta**: the **Tribunal de las Aguas** has adjudicated irrigation
    disputes at the cathedral's Apostles' Door for **over a thousand years** —
    water apportioned by proportional *turn*, monitored by elected *síndicos*,
    graduated sanctions for theft.
  - **Japanese *iriai*** common forests: thousands of villages rationing fuel,
    fodder and thatch by seasonal opening dates, work-groups (*kumi*), graduated
    fines, and hired *detectives* (monitors), stable since the Tokugawa era.
  - **Maine lobster gangs**: informal harbor gangs control entry; "perambulators"
    cut the trap-lines of outsiders; the v-notch norm spares egg-bearing females.
    The stock *rose* for decades while other fisheries collapsed.
  - **Alanya** (Turkish inshore fishery): a fisher-designed **lottery-rotation**
    of named fishing spots eliminated the race-to-fish with no state and no
    privatization.

These institutions did **not** take either of Hardin's exits. They are **neither**
privatization (no single owner, no alienable title) **nor** an external state
Leviathan. They are the **third thing**: self-governing commons, legitimate and
durable, that FDK's ontology forbids.

## 2. The eight design principles — and FDK's score

Ostrom's eight design principles (1990, Table 3.1) are her positive theory of *how*
the tragedy is escaped. For each, what it requires and whether FDK can represent it.

| # | Principle | Requires | FDK represents it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clearly defined boundaries | who may withdraw; the resource's edge | **No** (only in name). FDK's "boundary" is single-owner *title*, not *membership* in a commons. It has the word, not the concept. |
| 2 | Congruence (rules fit local conditions; benefit ∝ cost) | proportional, place-specific rules | **No.** One global rule (consent-of-owner); cannot localize or proportion. |
| 3 | Collective-choice arrangements | the affected can *modify* the rules | **No** — and this is the deepest clash. FDK's axioms are *frozen*; appropriators cannot self-legislate. |
| 4 | Monitoring | monitors accountable to users | **No.** FDK reads `coerced`/`deceived` as attested booleans; no monitoring primitive exists. |
| 5 | Graduated sanctions | escalating penalties | **No.** FDK is binary (ALLOW/DENY). The *iriai* fines, the line-cutting, the Valencia penalties — all unrepresentable. |
| 6 | Conflict-resolution mechanisms | cheap local arbitration (the Tribunal) | **No.** FDK *defers to a human* but models no standing forum. |
| 7 | Minimal recognition of the right to organize | external powers don't override local self-rule | **No** — FDK *is* the external power that overrides: any binding collective rule binds dissenters ⇒ DENY. |
| 8 | Nested enterprises | polycentric layering | **No.** FDK is flat and single-owner; no nesting. |

**Score: zero of eight, in any non-trivial sense.** FDK shares the *word*
"boundaries" with principle 1 and the *concept* of none of them. The eight
principles are the validated mechanism of legitimate collective ownership — the
exact object FDK declares unrepresentable — and FDK encodes not one.

## 3. The central kill

Strip the catalogue to its load-bearing claim. A durable commons is **constituted**
by acts that FDK's gate must DENY:

- **It binds a dissenting minority.** The wintering rule binds the Törbel household
  that wants more cows; the Alanya rotation binds the fisher who drew a bad spot;
  the catch cap binds the lobsterman who would set more traps. There is always a
  commoner who, *at this act*, does not consent — and the institution binds them
  anyway. To FDK that binding crosses a non-consenting boundary ⇒ **coercion /
  confiscation ⇒ DENY**.
- **It monitors without per-act consent.** The *iriai* detectives, the Valencia
  *síndicos*, the Maine perambulators surveil and sanction appropriators who never
  consented to *this* inspection. FDK has no monitoring primitive and would read
  the intrusion as a boundary crossing without consent.
- **It self-legislates.** The appropriators *make and change* the rules that bind
  them, by ongoing membership rather than per-act consent (principle 3). FDK's gate
  is fixed law; it cannot represent a body that writes its own.

Each of these is the **constitutive act** of every long-lived commons in §1. FDK
does not merely fail to *bless* them; asked to legitimize them, it **DENIES** them.
And the alternative FDK *would* bless — unlimited individual extraction (§0.1) — is
the very behaviour those institutions exist to prevent, the behaviour that, left
ungoverned, collapsed the North Atlantic cod.

So the indictment sharpens past "silence":

> On the commons FDK is not silent. It emits a **determinate, false pair**:
> *unrestrained extraction is legitimate; conservation is confiscation.* That is
> Hardin's prediction, and Ostrom showed Hardin's prediction is false for thousands
> of real commons. FDK is wrong **in the specific direction a Nobel laureate
> empirically disproved**, and it encodes **zero** of the eight principles by which
> the disproof operates.

## 4. The death-condition (written first, as a falsifier must be)

> **If durable, legitimate commons require non-consensual minority-binding — i.e.
> if the constitutive act of a long-lived common-pool institution (binding a
> dissenting commoner to a cap, monitoring without per-act consent, self-legislated
> rule-change) is genuinely legitimate — then FDK's gate, which DENIES exactly that
> act as coercion/confiscation, returns *false* for the commons. On the commons,
> FDK is false.**

State it in one line, the form the program demands:

> **If durable legitimate commons require non-consensual minority-binding, FDK's
> gate is false for the commons.**

What would it take for FDK to **survive** this? It must show the antecedent is
false in the relevant sense — that the binding is **not** truly non-consensual:

- **(R) The revocability rescue.** Perhaps the commoner consents *to the
  constitution* (a revocable membership with a preserved exit) rather than to each
  act, and so the cap binds them by their own standing consent, not by coercion.
  This is the most promising thread — it is the same exit-right lever that carries
  the program's one ✚ (lock-in, `06`), now turned outward at the commons. It says:
  a commons rule is legitimate iff every member retains a *real exit* (leave the
  village, sell the parcel, quit the gang).
  **But the rescue is fragile on three points:**
  1. **It re-imports node G.** "Real exit" needs the exit-cost threshold. For a
     Törbel household whose entire livelihood is the alp, exit is ruin — exactly
     the case where FDK cannot yet say whether exit is real (`00`, node G). Until
     G is closed, (R) is schematic.
  2. **It may collapse into majority-confiscation.** If "you may dissent but not
     exit, so the rule binds you" is legitimate, what distinguishes it from the tax
     FDK proudly DENIES (`06`, taxation case)? Both bind a dissenter who has not
     left. If (R) blesses the commons cap it threatens to bless the tax; if it
     denies the tax it threatens to deny the cap. FDK needs a principled line and
     does not yet have one.
  3. **It does not deliver monitoring or graduated sanctions.** Even granting (R),
     principles 4 and 5 remain unrepresentable — a binary, attested-boolean gate
     has no surveillance and no escalation primitive. (R) at best rescues
     principle 3, leaving seven unaddressed.
- **(I) The institutional-layer rescue.** Perhaps FDK sits *under* an Ostromian
  institution that has already manufactured single-owner consents, deciding only
  legitimacy once the commons has constituted itself. **This fails directly:** the
  institution's *defining acts* (binding the dissenter, monitoring, rule-change)
  are the acts FDK would DENY if asked to legitimize them. FDK cannot sit under a
  commons whose constitution it rules illegitimate.

## 5. The three admissible outcomes

This kill, like Sen's, has three honest exits — and the program must accept whichever
the argument forces, not the one it prefers:

1. **FDK is wrong on the commons (the kill lands).** If neither (R) nor (I) holds,
   the death-condition's antecedent is true and FDK's gate is *false* for the
   commons — not scope-limited, false. The exit-right program's distinctive region
   is then bounded to the *bilateral* cases (lock-in, manufactured consent) and
   explicitly excludes collective ownership. That is a real result.
2. **FDK is rescued by revocability (R), at a price.** If the membership-with-exit
   reconstruction works, FDK survives — but only by closing node G *and* by drawing
   the commons/tax line, and it still represents only principle 3. "Survives" here
   means "survives partially, having paid for it." The honest report would be: FDK
   handles the *self-legislating* face of the commons and remains blind to its
   *monitoring/sanction* face.
3. **The cases are not really FDK's domain (the orthogonality plea).** If one insists
   FDK decides *legitimacy* and Ostrom describes *institutions*, the two never meet.
   But §3 and rescue (I) close this exit: the institution's constitutive acts are
   legitimacy-questions, and FDK answers them DENY. The plea is unavailable.

The director's standing instruction applies: a clean kill is worth more than a
defended survival. The point of this file is to make the commons kill *sharp enough
to land* — so that if FDK survives it, the survival means something.

## 6. Why this is the harder kill

Sen's contented slave (`03`) attacks where FDK is *quiet* — it under-protects a
person who asserts they need no protection, and FDK can at least say "I decline to
overrule a sincere will." On the commons FDK has no such refuge: it *speaks*, and
what it says (extract freely; conservation is theft) is the position history
labelled *the enclosure* and economics labelled *the tragedy*. A theory can perhaps
survive being silent. It is far harder to survive being **loud and wrong in the
direction the field's Nobel Prize was awarded for refuting**.

If the program is to keep its one candidate contribution (the preserved exit-right),
it must show that exit-right is the *same* lever that legitimizes a commons-by-
revocable-membership (R) — turning the Ostrom kill into the Ostrom *vindication*.
That is the single most valuable open thread out of this file, and it is unproven.
Until it is proven, the honest standing of FDK on the commons is the death-condition,
unrefuted.
