# Case Bank 01 — Voluntary Slavery, Self-Binding, and Labor Lock-In

**Thesis under test (RC):** Valid consent requires a *preserved, real, exercisable exit right*.
**Rubric (applied identically to every row):** for each case give Rothbard / Sen / Pettit verdicts, then the FDK-RC verdict (ALLOW iff a *real* exit remains), then which rival FDK *collapses to*, then whether it is a genuine **fourth route** (different from all three AND defensible without importing welfare / autonomy / non-domination).
**Decision rule:** exit FORECLOSED by the arrangement → RC=DENY, but only via an imported person-premise (collapse to Rothbard's inalienability or Pettit). Exit RETAINED-but-unwanted (contented case) → RC=ALLOW (=Rothbard), unless denied by judging her preference (=Sen). Fourth route expected ≈ never; any candidate is flagged loudly below.

*All verdicts are contestable reconstructions of each author's position, not quotations. They are reconstructed to apply the paper's `three_questions.md` / `exit_right.md` findings consistently, not to flatter FDK.*

## Reading of the pattern

Across all 21 cases the verdict structure predicted by `three_questions.md` holds without exception:

- **Foreclosed-exit cases** (1, 6, 7-clause, 9, 10, 12–18). RC returns DENY — but it only *fires* by importing a person-premise. Where the foreclosure is fraud/theft (14, 16) or inalienability (1, 6, 10), the import is **Rothbard**; where it is unchecked standing-power over a content party (12, 13, 17, 18), the import is **Pettit**. In every such row the "FDK reason" is one of the three rivals wearing consent-shaped notation. No new ground.
- **Retained-but-unwanted cases** (5, 11, and the contented edge of 19). RC's letter is *satisfied* (exit exists), so RC must say ALLOW — which is **Rothbard**. To reach the intuitively illegitimate DENY you must rule the available exit "not real *for her*" by judging her preference — which is **Sen**. RC has no third move here.
- **One-off transfers** (8, kidney sale) and **completed transfers** dissolve the question: with no ongoing arrangement there is nothing to exit, so RC is simply silent and defers to Rothbard.
- **Cost-not-foreclosure cases** (15 industry-wide vs firm, 19 golden handcuffs, 20 arbitration). These expose **Node G** (how much cost defeats an exit?) and the **exit-vs-voice gap**: RC indexes to *exit*, so it under-reaches where the real loss is *contestation* (20) — exactly where Pettit out-performs it.

The contented-slave row (#11) is where a fourth route would have to appear, and it is flagged below precisely because it is the one place the temptation is real.

---

## The one case that comes closest (flagged loudly, then cut)

**Case #11, the retained-exit contented slave**, is the only candidate worth pausing on, because it is built to sit *past* exit-availability — in the wanting. One could try to manufacture a fourth route by saying: "the *standing* to withdraw is structurally intact, so RC ALLOWs, yet the arrangement is a *relationship of subjection* whose very form is suspect." But that last clause is not a fact about her *consent or her exit* — it is a fact about the *relationship's power structure*, which is exactly **Pettit's domination**. The moment RC reaches for it, it has left consent-internal ground and imported non-domination. The alternative — DENY because her endorsement is deformed — is **Sen**. RC's own letter, applied honestly, returns **ALLOW (= Rothbard)**. So #11 is *not* a fourth route; it is the sharpest demonstration of why none exists: the residue RC would need is precisely the imported premise Q2 identified.

A weaker near-miss is **#20 (arbitration clause)**: here FDK's verdict (ALLOW on exit) actually *diverges* from Pettit/Sen (DENY) — but this is a divergence by **under-reaching**, not a novel correct verdict. RC simply does not see the loss of contestation. Divergence-by-blindness is not a fourth route; it is a defect.

## Mini-tally

- **Cases examined:** 21 (Cluster A: 11; Cluster B: 10).
- **Genuine fourth routes found:** **0.** No case yields a verdict that is (a) different from all three rivals AND (b) defensible without importing welfare, autonomy, or non-domination.
- **Cases that came close, and why they fail:**
  - **#11 retained-exit contented slave** — the designed hard case; collapses to Rothbard (ALLOW) on RC's letter, or to Sen/Pettit only by importing a person-premise.
  - **#20 arbitration clause** — FDK *diverges* from Pettit/Sen, but by missing the harm (voice/contestation), not by a defensible novel verdict; a defect, not a route.
- **Verdict consistent with `three_questions.md`:** foreclosed cases collapse to Rothbard or Pettit; retained-exit cases collapse to Rothbard or Sen. The case bank reproduces the paper's Q3 finding empirically across 21 cases and finds **no consent-internal fourth route.**
