# Case Bank 02 — Platform/Digital Lock-in and Labor/Contract Lock-in

> **Thesis under test (RC):** valid consent requires a *preserved exit right*. **Rubric:** for each case give Rothbard (bare consent), Sen (adaptive-preference / interest-judgment), Pettit (non-domination), then FDK-RC (does a real, exercisable exit remain?), what FDK *collapses-to*, and whether a genuine **fourth route** exists. **Fourth route = Y only if** FDK diverges from all three AND is defensible *without* importing self-ownership/welfare/autonomy/domination — flagged loudly if found. **Reading rule:** foreclosed exit ⇒ RC=DENY but only via an imported person-premise (collapse to Rothbard/Pettit); retained-but-unwanted exit ⇒ RC=ALLOW (=Rothbard) unless denied by judging the preference (=Sen).

These verdicts are contestable reconstructions, not citations. Rivals are given their strongest form. The live probe for this cluster: digital lock-in is where "preserved exit" is most *concrete* (data portability, switching costs, egress fees) — so it is the best place to ask whether RC adds anything to non-domination, or merely operationalizes it.

## Row notes (load-bearing reasoning only)

**1–4, 8, 10, 13–15, 17 — the foreclosure family.** In each, the arrangement removes a *real* exit: no data/contact/document portability, no rival distribution channel, no repair path, a barred occupational field, eviction-on-quit, unappealable deactivation. RC fires and reaches DENY. But per Q2, RC only earns DENY here by importing a premise about why the *foreclosed* exit matters — and that premise *is* non-domination (you are now subject to the counterparty's arbitrary power over your graph/field/livelihood). Pettit reaches the identical verdict and reaches it more directly, because he indexes to the *structure of power*, not to a present consent token. **Collapse to Pettit; no fourth route.**

**5 (dark-pattern cancel), 9 (carrier lock).** These are foreclosure-by-friction: the exit "exists" formally but its exercise is obstructed. RC's "real, exercisable" clause does work Rothbard's bare consent will not — Rothbard sees a cancel link and says ALLOW. But the work RC does is exactly Pettit's point that *arbitrary obstruction of exit is domination*; and the residual ("the friction exploits inattention") is Sen's. No daylight.

**6 (smart-home brick), 3/12 (egress fee, exit tax).** Cases 3 and 12 are pure **Node G** (exit-cost threshold): RC cannot return a verdict until it says *how* costly an exit must be to count as foreclosed — and any such threshold smuggles in either a welfare baseline (Sen) or a non-arbitrariness test on the fee (Pettit). Case 6 is starker: exit *destroys the asset*, so it is foreclosure dressed as availability ⇒ Pettit.

**7 (engagement feed), 11 (loot-box) — the retained-exit contented cases.** Nothing is removed; the door is open; the *wanting* is engineered. RC's letter is satisfied (exit preserved) ⇒ RC must return **ALLOW** = Rothbard. To deny, RC must declare the available exit "not real *for her*" because her preference is deformed = Sen. This is Node K inside the cluster: RC has *no structural residue* once exit is retained. **Collapse to Rothbard (permit) or Sen (deny). No fourth route — and this is the decisive negative.**

**16 (unpaid internship), 18 (mandatory arbitration).** 18 is instructive: RC's most natural reading condemns it because the clause strips the *contestation* mechanism — but "exit + contestation that makes power trackable" is the **definition of Pettit's secured standing**, not a consent-internal discovery. 16 is mostly Node G (is the sunk hope a ruinous exit cost?) layered over Sen (exploited aspiration).

**19 (up-or-out), 20 (control).** Both converge on ALLOW across all theories. 19 matters as a discriminator: an *ex-ante, non-arbitrary, escapable* rule is exactly what Pettit counts as **non-dominating**, and RC agrees because exit is preserved — confirming that RC and non-domination move together. 20 is the clean control: a freely-terminable contract is RC-valid, Rothbard-valid, Pettit-non-dominated, Sen-fine. RC adds nothing here precisely *because* it adds nothing wrong here.

## Mini-tally — genuine fourth routes

**Fourth routes found: 0 / 20.** Every row collapses to Rothbard (retained-but-unwanted exit: 7, 11, 16, 19, 20), to Pettit (foreclosed exit: 1–6, 8–10, 13–15, 17, 18), or — on the engineered-wanting residue — to Sen (5, 7, 11). RC never diverges from all three while staying defensible without an imported premise. This *confirms* the paper's Q3 verdict at scale: on the cases that decide the project, FDK has no consent-internal fourth route.

## The single case where RC most plausibly adds something — and whether it really does

The strongest candidate is **#1 (no data export)** — and more sharply, the **data-portability axis** running through 1, 2, 4. This is where "preserved exit" is most concrete and machine-checkable: a *right to export your data in a usable format* is a crisp, operational exit condition with no appeal to welfare or to the user's inner wanting. RC seems to earn its keep: Rothbard sees "you agreed to the EULA" and permits; RC sees an exit foreclosed *in fact* and denies — a real verdict-difference from bare consent, with no interest-judgment imported.

Does RC therefore *add* something beyond non-domination? Honestly: **no — it implements it.** The reason a non-portable social graph is illegitimate is that it leaves the user subject to the platform's *arbitrary, unexercised-but-standing* power over their relationships and history — which is Pettit's domination, exactly. RC contributes the **operationalization** (portability as the measurable proxy for "secured exit"), not the **ground** (why foreclosed exit is illegitimate at all). That ground still has to be imported, and on Q2's menu the only non-welfare, non-self-ownership option that fits the data case is non-domination. So data portability is RC's best case and it is *still* Pettit wearing a consent-shaped, engineering-friendly notation: a genuine contribution to *enforcement*, not to *theory*. The fourth route does not appear even here — which is the most informative possible result, because here is where it should have.
