# Killer-Test 04 — Social / Membership / Intimate (the act-locus crack)

> **The hunt.** One case that revives FDK: **Nozick PERMIT** (consent valid, entitlements clean)
> AND **Pettit PERMIT** (no agent now holds arbitrary power) AND **Sen PERMIT** (capabilities
> intact) BUT **FDK FORBID**, by an **independent** reason — *not* Rothbard inalienability-of-will,
> *not* Pettit domination, *not* Sen capability, *not* non-identity (future people don't count for FDK).
> **The crack:** FDK indexes **acts**; rivals index **states/relations**. So the candidate site is the
> *act of designing a structure that forecloses future exit* — evaluable even when no runtime state is bad.
> **The danger:** if FDK's reason is really about *irreversible institutional design* (no-exit rule-building),
> it RELOCATES to governance ethics and does **not** revive FDK *as a freedom theory*.

**Rubric per case:** state it; Nozick / Pettit / Sen verdicts (target all PERMIT; any FORBID = fail, say so);
FDK verdict + reason; **CLASSIFY:** SURVIVES (flag LOUDLY) / COLLAPSES-TO [Rothbard / Pettit / Sen / non-identity / FDK-silent] / RELOCATES (independent reason but institutional-design, not freedom).

---

## S1 — The consensual no-exit commune, founder departed

A group of competent adults found an intentional community. The charter, fixed at t₀ and
**architecturally irrevocable** (no amendment clause; property pooled into a perpetual trust
whose deed forbids dissolution; the rule binds *the structure*, not persons), provides that
membership, once taken, cannot be renounced — there is no procedure to leave, by design.
The founder, having authored it, dies. At runtime: members thrive, no leader holds power, no
one is interfered with, all consented as adults.

- **Nozick:** PERMIT — valid t₀ consent, clean title in the trust. ✓
- **Pettit:** PERMIT (strained, but holds) — domination is a *standing relation to an agent
  with capacity to interfere*; an authorless trust-deed has no such agent at runtime. ✓
- **Sen:** PERMIT — capabilities are met; members flourish. (If we say "no-exit *itself* is a
  capability loss," Sen FORBIDS and the test fails — see note.) ✓ (contested)
- **FDK:** FORBID — the **act** of designing a structure that forecloses future exit crosses the
  exit-boundary of every future-member-self; the design is illegitimate *to enter/author*, not
  merely unenforceable to hold.
- **CLASSIFY:** **COLLAPSES-TO Rothbard.** This is exactly the red-team's reopened-then-closed
  case (`red_team_the_verdict.md` §3): designed-then-autonomous foreclosure. FDK's "you cannot
  author away your own future exit" **is** Rothbard's inalienability-of-will, reached by another
  name. The founder is dead, but each *member* who joined irrevocably is a consenting adult
  self-binding — Rothbard frees them (no alienable title to the will moved). No fourth route.

## S2 — Citizenship/nationality regime with no renunciation

A polity's founding law makes citizenship **perpetual and non-renounceable**: you may emigrate
physically, but you can never shed the status, its tax-and-conscription claims follow you
worldwide, and the law was ratified by the founding generation's genuine consent. Present
administration is non-arbitrary, rule-bound, contestable (Pettit-clean); citizens are prosperous
(Sen-clean); current adults consented or are bound by a clean constitutional chain.

- **Nozick:** PERMIT *only if* the chain to present persons is consensual; for those who never
  consented it is already a Nozick problem. Restrict to the founders who ratified: PERMIT. ✓
- **Pettit:** **FORBID.** A *worldwide enforceable claim* (tax, conscription) IS a standing
  capacity to interfere held by an agent (the state). The state is a runtime dominator. ✗ **FAIL.**
- **Sen:** borderline-FORBID (the follow-you-worldwide claim narrows the option set).
- **FDK:** FORBID.
- **CLASSIFY:** **COLLAPSES-TO Pettit** — and **fails the test** (Pettit forbids). A citizenship
  *trap* with live enforcement always has a present dominator. To kill the dominator you must
  remove enforcement — but then it is not a trap. Citizenship regimes cannot escape Pettit.

## S3 — Closed mutual-aid society, no withdrawal of one's pooled stake

A friendly-society / takaful-style mutual: members pool capital for lifelong mutual insurance.
The bylaws, fixed at founding, make the **pooled stake non-withdrawable** — you may stop
*receiving* benefits and walk away socially, but your contributed capital is permanently
collectivized; there is, by design, no exit-with-your-stake, ever. Governance is flat and
non-arbitrary; members are well-served; all joined as adults.

- **Nozick:** PERMIT — you *gave* your stake under valid terms; it is now the pool's title.
  Walking away costs you property you alienated, which is fine. ✓
- **Pettit:** PERMIT — you can leave the relationship; no agent holds arbitrary power over your
  *person*; the pool is a thing, not a master. ✓
- **Sen:** PERMIT — capabilities intact; you keep every option except clawing back a *thing*. ✓
- **FDK:** Tries to FORBID ("stake-exit foreclosed") — but here exit *of the person* is fully
  preserved; only **property** is non-returnable.
- **CLASSIFY:** **COLLAPSES-TO Rothbard (FDK actually PERMITS).** This is the clean Rothbard
  alienable/inalienable split: external title *can* be irrevocably transferred; the will cannot.
  Since only the *thing* is locked and the *person* walks free, FDK's exit-right is satisfied →
  PERMIT. No divergence. (Useful control: foreclosing *property*-exit never triggers FDK.)

## S4 — The clan covenant that binds only descendants

A founding generation erects a clan structure — names, inheritance, ritual obligations — whose
rules foreclose exit **for those born into it**: a child of the clan is, by design, a member for
life with no renunciation procedure. The founders themselves consented; they bind not each other
but the **not-yet-born**.

- **Nozick / Pettit / Sen:** various, but —
- **CLASSIFY (disqualified):** **COLLAPSES-TO non-identity.** The bound parties are future people,
  who **do not count for FDK** (stated constraint). Whatever wrong is here is a future-generations
  wrong, a site FDK explicitly collapses on. **Out of scope by construction** — listed only to mark
  the trap: every "bind the descendants" framing dies here, not at a fourth route.

## S5 — The permanent-embed reputation ledger (the act-locus's best shot)

A founding cohort designs a **closed reputation protocol** for a social world (a guild, a
profession, a diaspora network). By design, the ledger is **append-only and non-erasable**: once
you are entered, your standing is permanently embedded; there is **no procedure, ever, to remove
yourself from the registry** — not to falsify entries, simply to *not be enrolled*. Crucially:
(a) enrollment of present members was *consented* (Nozick-clean); (b) the protocol runs
autonomously — the founders are gone, no administrator can alter or weaponize it, it merely
*persists* (Pettit-clean: no agent with capacity to interfere); (c) members are not harmed,
slandered, or capability-deprived — entries are accurate, the network is benign, they flourish
(Sen-clean). The only thing foreclosed is the bare standing to **not be a permanently-indexed
participant** — exit from *being-enrolled-at-all*.

- **Nozick:** PERMIT — consented enrollment, accurate records, no rights violated. ✓
- **Pettit:** PERMIT — no agent holds arbitrary power; the ledger is authorless and inert; no one
  can flip a switch against you. The append-only protocol *dominates no one* in Pettit's modal
  sense (no capacity-to-interfere is *held* by anyone). ✓
- **Sen:** PERMIT — capabilities are fully intact; accurate benign embedding removes no option the
  member has reason to value. ✓
- **FDK:** FORBID — the **act of designing** an enrollment that can never be exited forecloses the
  member's standing to withdraw the *fact of participation*. The wrong is in authoring the
  no-exit architecture, indexed at the design **act**, not at any runtime relation.
- **Is the reason independent?** Test it against each escape:
  - *Rothbard?* Rothbard frees the **person** but lets you irrevocably transfer **things/data**.
    A reputation *entry* is alienated information — closer to a transferred title than to the will.
    Rothbard would say: you consented to be recorded; the record is now the network's, like a
    published fact; you cannot un-publish it; no inalienable-will claim is touched. So Rothbard
    **PERMITS** — and here FDK's FORBID does **not** reduce to inalienability-of-will. *This is the
    one place the case slips Rothbard.* ⟵ genuinely interesting.
  - *Pettit?* No runtime dominator (stipulated, and the stipulation holds for an inert ledger). ✓ escaped.
  - *Sen?* No capability loss (accurate, benign). ✓ escaped.
  - *Non-identity?* The bound party is a present consenting adult, not a future person. ✓ escaped.
- **So is it SURVIVES?** **No — it RELOCATES, and barely escapes COLLAPSES-TO-FDK-silent.** Two
  killing objections:
  1. **FDK-silent / no-actor.** FDK's gate needs an **actor whose boundary-crossing it denies**
     (`reverse_test.md` §3). At runtime there is *no* actor — the ledger is authorless and inert,
     exactly the agentless-foreclosure box where "RC is silent." To get a verdict FDK must reach
     **back** to the t₀ design act. But the t₀ designer *did* obtain consent and is now gone, so
     the only boundary "crossed" is against the member's *own later self* — which is **self-binding**,
     and self-binding of a present adult is Rothbard's turf again *unless* the thing bound is the
     will. Whether a permanent reputation-entry binds the **will** (→ Rothbard catches it, COLLAPSE)
     or merely a **thing** (→ Rothbard permits, FDK alone forbids) is the entire knife-edge.
  2. **If it is a thing → the reason is institutional-design, not freedom.** Suppose we grant the
     entry is a *thing* (Rothbard permits). Then FDK's residual FORBID is: "*it is wrong to build an
     irreversible no-exit registry.*" That is a verdict about the **ethics of designing permanent
     institutions** — append-only ledgers, lock-in protocols, no-sunset rules. It is governance/
     rules-design ethics (closer to Ostrom/constitutional-design than to legitimate consent). The
     reason is **independent** of all three rivals, **but it is not a reason about freedom** — it is
     a reason about *irreversibility of built structures*. **RELOCATES.**
- **CLASSIFY:** **RELOCATES** (independent reason, but institutional/irreversibility ethics, not a
  freedom-of-the-person theory). The act-locus is *real* — FDK genuinely evaluates the design act
  where the rivals (state/relation-indexed) are quiet — **but the evaluated property is
  irreversibility-of-design, not domination/capability/consent of a free person.** It revives FDK
  as a *theory of legitimate institutional design*, **not** as the freedom theory it claims to be.

## S6 — Intimate: the irrevocable vow with no counterparty power

Two adults design a covenant marriage whose terms foreclose dissolution **by architecture**
(escrowed mutual property in a deed that self-destructs on any divorce filing, making exit
materially impossible), then both sincerely *want* the permanence. Neither holds power over the
other; both flourish.

- **Nozick:** PERMIT (consented). **Pettit:** PERMIT (symmetric — neither party dominates; the
  *deed* binds both equally, no agent has one-sided capacity). **Sen:** borderline-PERMIT.
- **FDK:** FORBID (irrevocable foreclosure of exit).
- **CLASSIFY:** **COLLAPSES-TO Rothbard.** Two consenting adults self-binding their *own* future —
  the paradigm inalienability-of-will case. Pettit is escaped (symmetry kills the dominator), but
  Rothbard catches it cleanly. No fourth route.

## Tally

- **Cases:** 6 (S4 disqualified as non-identity; S2 fails on Pettit).
- **SURVIVES (independent fourth freedom-route): 0.**
- **RELOCATES: 1** (S5 — the strongest case).
- **COLLAPSES-TO:** Rothbard ×3 (S1, S3→permit, S6); Pettit ×1 (S2, also fails); non-identity ×1 (S4).

## Strongest case and the honest verdict

**S5 (permanent-embed reputation ledger) is the strongest** — it is the only social case that
clears Nozick **and** Pettit **and** Sen **and** non-identity simultaneously, and whose FDK-FORBID
does **not** obviously reduce to inalienability-of-will (a reputation *entry* reads as an alienated
*thing*, which Rothbard permits transferring irrevocably). That is exactly the act-locus crack the
red-team flagged: FDK speaks at the **design act** where the relation-indexed rivals fall silent.

But it does **not revive FDK as a freedom theory.** It splits on a knife-edge and loses both ways.
If the embedded entry binds the **will**, Rothbard catches it (COLLAPSE). If it binds only a
**thing**, then FDK's residual reason — "do not author irreversible no-exit structures" — is a
verdict about the **ethics of irreversible institutional design**, not about the freedom of a
person: no one is dominated, deprived, or coerced; the only defect is that a *built structure*
cannot be undone. That is governance/constitutional-design territory (the no-sunset, append-only,
unamendable-charter family), independent of the three rivals but **categorically not a theory of
legitimate consent or non-domination.** The act-locus is genuine; the property it indexes is
**irreversibility**, not **freedom**. **RELOCATES — FDK is not revived.**

The deeper finding the social domain forces: every attempt to make the foreclosure *bite a person*
(S2 enforcement, S6 self-binding) drags in a runtime dominator (Pettit) or self-binding adult
(Rothbard); the *only* way to keep all three rivals silent is to make the foreclosed thing
**inert and impersonal** (S5's authorless ledger) — at which point the wrong is no longer about a
person's freedom at all, but about the irreversibility of a structure. The crack is real and it is
**closed against freedom**: the same move that escapes Pettit/Rothbard/Sen also escapes *freedom
itself*, landing in design ethics. No social/membership/intimate case revives FDK.
