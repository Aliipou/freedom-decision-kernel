# Killer Test 03 — Economic / Market / Infrastructure Domain

> **The hunt.** One case revives FDK iff: **Nozick = PERMIT** (valid consent, no rights
> violated) AND **Pettit = PERMIT** (no agent holding arbitrary power *now*) AND **Sen =
> PERMIT** (capabilities intact) BUT **FDK = FORBID**, with FDK's reason *independent* —
> not Rothbard inalienability-of-will, not Pettit domination, not Sen capability, not the
> non-identity/future-generations problem.
>
> **The crack being probed (red_team_the_verdict §3, §"least sure"):** FDK indexes **acts**;
> rivals index **states/relations**. Is *the ACT of designing a structure that forecloses
> future exit* a site of evaluation none of the three captures — even with no runtime
> dominator? FDK would FORBID the **design act** itself.
>
> **The relocation trap (flagged hard up front):** "lock-in" is a *fully developed economic
> concept* — David's QWERTY, Arthur's increasing returns, North on institutions, Klemperer/
> Farrell-Shapiro switching costs. If FDK's distinctive reason turns out to be "don't
> engineer irreversible lock-in," that is **mechanism design / institutional economics**, not
> a theory of *freedom*, and it RELOCATES FDK out of legitimacy theory rather than reviving
> it. **Emergent** lock-in is worse for FDK still: with no foreclosing *act*, FDK has nothing
> to condemn and is **SILENT** (not a win). This domain is where relocation risk is highest.

Verdicts are contestable reconstructions; rivals given strongest form. Aim: all three PERMIT.

---

## E1 — The designed monetary union with no exit clause (the strongest economic candidate)

**State it.** Sovereign states (or, to dodge non-identity, *currently-existing* citizens via
referendum) ratify a monetary union whose founding treaty is drafted to contain **no exit
mechanism**: surrendering the national currency, central bank, and monetary sovereignty is
engineered at t₀ to be legally and technically irreversible. Years later a member's economy
needs devaluation it cannot perform. No agent commands it to stay; the treaty runs itself.

| Rival | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nozick | **PERMIT** | Valid ratification, no rights violated, entitlements transferred by consent at t₀. |
| Pettit | **PERMIT** (strained) | At runtime *no agent* holds arbitrary power to interfere — the union is a rule, authorless, abandoned by its drafters. Pettit must stretch "domination" to an authorless structure or fall silent. |
| Sen | **PERMIT** (contestable) | Capabilities arguably intact at ratification; the deprivation is a later macro-shock, agentless — Sen's *blight* cell, not a t₀ wrong. |
| **FDK** | **FORBID** | The **design act** that foreclosed exit is illegitimate independent of any runtime dominator. |

**FDK's reason, and where it actually lands.** FDK condemns the *act of authoring an
exit-less structure*. Is that reason independent? Walk it:
- **Not non-identity** — we used currently-existing ratifiers, so this is dodged. Good.
- **Not Pettit** — genuine: no runtime agent. This is a real win *over Pettit*.
- **Not Nozick** — genuine: consent was valid. Real win *over Nozick*.
- **But what is the reason FORBID is *right*?** It is that a *future* member, facing the
  macro-shock, has no exit. That future-member's complaint is **a capability complaint
  deferred in time** (she cannot devalue → cannot secure livelihood) OR a **Rothbard
  complaint** if we say the polity cannot alienate its *standing* will to govern itself.
  FDK's "the design act foreclosed exit" only bites because *foreclosed exit is bad* — and
  the ground for *why* is supplied from outside: Sen (the later deprivation) or Rothbard
  (inalienable self-governance). FDK contributes the **act-locus** (condemn t₀, not t₁), not
  the **ground**.

**CLASSIFY: RELOCATES.** The independent element FDK adds is real but it is *temporal/
structural*: "do not author irreversible foreclosure." That is **lock-in economics applied to
monetary regimes** (optimum-currency-area exit costs, hysteresis) plus an imported reason for
why foreclosure is *bad*. The freedom-content is borrowed; the novel content is mechanism
design. **Not a revival.**

## E2 — Critical infrastructure built to trap a region (single-pipe dependency)

**State it.** A firm builds the *only* viable water/energy/data conduit into a region, under
a freely-signed concession, deliberately engineered with no interconnect so the region cannot
ever switch suppliers. The firm then sells the asset to a passive trust that merely collects
the contracted (non-arbitrary, fixed-by-formula) tariff. No one *exercises* power; the trap is
in the topology.

- **Nozick: PERMIT** — concession validly signed, tariff as agreed, no rights violated.
- **Pettit: FORBID.** This is the case's fatal flaw for the hunt. A single-supplier
  dependency where the supplier *retains the standing capacity* to raise the tariff, degrade
  service, or cut off — *even via the trust* — is paradigm **domination**: arbitrary power
  over a vulnerable party, exercised or not. Pettit fires immediately.

**CLASSIFY: CASE FAILS** (Pettit forbids). To make Pettit permit, the tariff/service must be
*fully fixed and self-enforcing with no residual discretion* — but then the "trap" is purely
the **topological switching cost**, i.e. emergent-once-built lock-in with no agent and no live
foreclosing act at runtime → and FDK's grip is again just **lock-in economics** (E1's
relocation) or, if no design act is isolable, **SILENT**. Either branch loses.

## E3 — Marketplace whose rules are set once and foreclose seller exit

**State it.** A platform founder writes immutable, on-chain marketplace rules at t₀ (fees,
listing terms, an irreversible "no off-platform solicitation of your own buyers" covenant),
renounces all admin keys, and leaves. Sellers join freely. At runtime the protocol has **no
operator** — keys are burned. A seller later wants to take her buyer relationships elsewhere;
the burned-in covenant forecloses it. No agent can change or enforce-by-choice; it just runs.

- **Nozick: PERMIT** — sellers consented to published immutable terms; no rights violated.
- **Pettit: PERMIT (strained, the genuine case).** With keys burned, *no agent* holds power
  to interfere arbitrarily. The constraint binds, but it binds *authorlessly*. This is the
  red-team's "designed-then-autonomous foreclosure" — Pettit is strained to silence.
- **Sen: PERMIT (contestable).** The seller keeps general market capability; she loses
  *these* buyer relationships, but capability-set is arguably intact (other channels exist).
- **FDK: FORBID** — the founder's **act** of burning an exit-foreclosing covenant into an
  ungovernable structure.

**The decisive question: is FDK's reason here independent of Rothbard?** The seller's
foreclosed exit is foreclosure of *her ongoing control over her own relationships/labor* — and
"you cannot irrevocably alienate authority over your own person/will" is **precisely
Rothbard's inalienability-of-will**, which the red-team already showed catches the
designed-autonomous case (red_team §3). FDK denies for the same partition Rothbard draws: exit
must be preserved for ongoing authority over the *person*, not for transfers of *things*. The
covenant binds the person's future action → Rothbard fires.

**CLASSIFY: COLLAPSES-TO Rothbard.** Distinct from Pettit and Nozick (real wins over both),
identical to Rothbard — i.e. exactly "projection of the rivals," confirmed on a harder case.
*If* we strip the personal-action element (covenant binds only an external listing asset, not
the seller's future conduct), Rothbard *permits* (external property is alienable) — and then
FDK has no reason to forbid either, or its reason is again the bare **lock-in** of switching
costs → **RELOCATES / SILENT**. No survivor on either branch.

## E4 — IP regime foreclosing follow-on creation (the cleanest separation attempt)

**State it.** A standards body, by unanimous consent of all *current* members, adopts a
perpetual, irrevocable IP regime: a foundational format/patent pool is locked so that
follow-on creators must forever build *within* it; the design deliberately forecloses any
future fork or alternative standard. No member is coerced; all consented; the body then
dissolves, leaving the regime self-perpetuating with no governing agent.

- **Nozick: PERMIT** — voluntary pooling of justly-held entitlements; no rights violated.
- **Pettit: PERMIT (strained).** Body dissolved → no agent with arbitrary power over
  follow-on creators *now*. (If a licensor retains discretionary licensing power, Pettit
  fires — so we stipulate fully-automatic, non-discretionary terms.)
- **Sen: PERMIT (contestable).** Follow-on creators retain broad capability; they are
  constrained to *one* substrate, but "can create within X" is a live capability.
- **FDK: FORBID** — the act of foreclosing the *fork*, i.e. exit from the standard.

**Is the reason about freedom, or path-dependence?** The wrong, if any, is that the standard
is **locked-in** and forks are foreclosed — this is *literally* Arthur's increasing-returns /
David's QWERTY lock-in and North's institutional path-dependence, in its home domain (IP/
standards). The follow-on creators who suffer are, crucially, **largely not-yet-existing** at
t₀ — and FDK *collapses on non-identity* (the brief's own exclusion): not-yet-existing creators
don't count as complainants. For the *currently-existing* would-be forker, her complaint is
"my capability to build an alternative is foreclosed" = **Sen**, or "the pool dominates the
field" = **Pettit** (if any discretion remains).

**CLASSIFY: RELOCATES (to lock-in economics) — with a non-identity leak.** The distinctive
reason is path-dependence/standard lock-in, the most developed form of the economic concept,
*not* freedom. Where it is not lock-in economics, it is Sen or non-identity. No fourth route.

## E5 — Control / discriminator: emergent dominant standard (no foreclosing act)

**State it.** A standard (QWERTY, a fiat reserve currency, a dominant file format) becomes
near-inescapable through **decentralized adoption** — increasing returns, network effects — but
*no one ever authored* a foreclosing rule. Switching is ruinously costly; exit is, in fact,
foreclosed. No design act exists.

- Nozick/Pettit/Sen: **PERMIT** (no rights violation, no agent, capability arguably intact).
- **FDK: SILENT.** There is **no act** to condemn — the foreclosure is emergent, not
  authored. FDK's whole gate needs an *actor* whose boundary-crossing it denies (reverse_test
  §3). An agentless, authorless lock-in offers FDK nothing to grip.

**CLASSIFY: FDK-SILENT-because-lock-in-is-emergent-not-an-act.** This is the load-bearing
discriminator: it shows FDK's *only* purchase in this domain is the presence of a **design
act** — and wherever that act exists (E1, E3, E4) the act's *wrongness* turns out to be
supplied by Rothbard, Sen, or by lock-in economics. The act-locus gives FDK a place to *point*,
never an independent reason to *condemn*.

## The honest residue (why the crack does not revive FDK *here*)

The act-locus crack is **real**: FDK genuinely *points* where the relation-theorists are
strained — at the t₀ design act of an exit-foreclosing structure that later runs without a
dominator (E1, E3, E4 all defeat Pettit and Nozick). That is not nothing; it is exactly the
red-team's "FDK speaks via the design act."

But pointing is not *grounding*. In every economic case the **reason the foreclosing act is
wrong** is imported: Rothbard (the act alienates the person's ongoing authority — E3), Sen (the
later, agentless deprivation — E1), or non-identity (the sufferers don't yet exist — E4). And
when none of those applies, the residue is **lock-in / path-dependence**, which is a mature
*economic* theory of why irreversible commitments are costly — **mechanism design, not
freedom.** So in the domain where lock-in is *best developed*, FDK's distinctive contribution
is most clearly **relocated out of legitimacy theory**: it is the *detector* of "where was
exit engineered shut" (the layer-D instrument of iteration_secured_exit §5), not an
independent *theory* of why that is illegitimate. The crack lets FDK aim; it never lets FDK
fire on its own ammunition.

**Strongest case:** E1 (exit-less monetary union with currently-existing ratifiers) — it best
defeats Pettit and Nozick and best dodges non-identity, and is therefore the most dangerous to
the negative verdict. It still RELOCATES: its independent content is optimum-currency-area exit
economics plus an imported ground for why foreclosed exit harms. **No revival in the economic
domain.**
