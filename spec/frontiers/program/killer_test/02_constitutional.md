# Killer Test — Constitutional / Political Domain

> **The hunt.** FDK indexes *acts* — a boundary-crossing at a time. The rivals index
> *states/relations*: Nozick (was consent/transfer valid?), Pettit (is anyone
> dominating *now*?), Sen (are capabilities limited?). The live crack from
> [`../../../paper/red_team_the_verdict.md`](../../../paper/red_team_the_verdict.md)
> §"locus of illegitimacy": **the ACT of designing a structure that forecloses future
> exit** may be a site of moral evaluation none of the three captures, because all three
> read the *runtime relation*, not the *founding act*.
>
> **The killer test.** Find a case where Nozick = PERMIT, Pettit = PERMIT, Sen = PERMIT,
> but FDK = FORBID — and FDK's reason is *independent*: not Rothbard's
> inalienability-of-will (self-binding), not Pettit's domination, not Sen's capability,
> not the non-identity / future-generations problem (FDK collapses there; not-yet-existing
> people do **not** count).
>
> **The danger.** Even if FDK forbids distinctively, is its reason about *freedom /
> legitimacy*, or about *irreversible institutional design* (Buchanan/Brennan,
> *The Reason of Rules*; North/Pierson path-dependence; entrenchment theory)? If the
> reason is "don't build irreversible lock-in," that is constitutional economics, not a
> theory of freedom, and FDK **RELOCATES** rather than survives.
>
> **Most promising sub-class:** *present dissenters bound by a founding they did not
> consent to.* This avoids non-identity (the bound parties exist now). The whole hunt
> turns on whether Pettit or Sen already catch the present dissenter — and whether FDK's
> reason, when it differs, is freedom or merely entrenchment.

*Verdicts below are contestable reconstructions, steelmanned for FDK and then attacked.*

---

## Case 1 — The eternity clause (unamendable constitutional article)

**Statement.** A constituent assembly, by a fair supermajority of *all then-living
adults*, ratifies a constitution one of whose articles declares itself **unamendable in
perpetuity** (e.g. Germany's Basic Law Art. 79(3) "eternity clause" entrenching human
dignity and the federal-democratic order; or a hypothetical clause entrenching a state
religion). The act under evaluation is **the founding act of entrenchment** — designing
the clause so no future majority can revise it. At runtime the order is mild,
non-interfering, and most citizens approve.

- **Nozick — PERMIT.** No entitlement is violated by writing an unamendable clause; no
  one's holdings are taken, no transfer is coerced. Founders may dispose of the
  constitutional text they authored. The future majority that *wants* to amend has no
  *entitlement* to a particular procedure — there is no Lockean right to a specific
  amendment rule. **PERMIT.**
- **Pettit — PERMIT (contested, but holds).** Is there a dominating agent at runtime? The
  clause runs autonomously; the founders are dead. No standing agent holds arbitrary power
  to interfere with the future majority — the *text* binds, not a person. Pettit can call
  a non-arbitrary, contestable constitutional order *non-dominating* even if rigid; an
  un-amendable but mild order with rule-of-law dominates no one. **PERMIT.** (If we said
  the clause *is* a standing dominator, the case fails — but an authorless rule is exactly
  the locus Pettit strains to reach; see red_team §"reopened candidate".)
- **Sen — PERMIT.** Capabilities are intact; citizens live well, are nourished, educated,
  can function. A fixed-but-benign rule does not reduce the capability set. **PERMIT.**
- **FDK — FORBID.** The founding act crossed the boundary of *every future dissenter* who
  did not consent and *cannot ever* end the future obligation: the clause forecloses exit
  from the rule in perpetuity. By the revocability clause of `Consent`
  ([`../00_definitions.md`](../00_definitions.md)), a binding that cannot be withdrawn
  alienates the exit right of the bound — and that exit right is not the *founders'* to
  alienate on others' behalf. **FORBID.**

**Adjudication.** Who is bound? Two populations. (a) *Future, not-yet-born* citizens — but
FDK **collapses to non-identity** here; not-yet-existing people do not count, by the
hunt's own rule. (b) *Present dissenters* who voted against ratification — they exist, did
not consent, and are bound forever. This is the promising sub-class. But examine FDK's
reason against the present dissenter. The dissenter *can leave the country* (emigrate) —
occurrent exit exists. FDK's forbid therefore cannot rest on "no exit *from the rule*"
unless exit means *exit from the rule without leaving the polity*, i.e. the standing to
revise the rule by ordinary politics. That is **the capability to self-govern** — and that
is **Sen's cell** (collectively-held political functioning), or, framed as "the majority
holds un-contestable power over the dissenter forever," it is **Pettit's** standing
arbitrary power of the entrenched order. The reason re-imports a rival.

**CLASSIFY: COLLAPSES-TO [Sen / Pettit].** FDK's distinctive-looking forbid against the
present dissenter dissolves on inspection: either the dissenter has occurrent exit
(emigration) and FDK should PERMIT, or "exit" is reinterpreted as the contestable power to
revise — which is Sen's capability-to-self-govern or Pettit's non-domination, not an
independent fourth route.

## Case 2 — The secession-barring federation (an entrenched union with no exit)

**Statement.** Sovereign polities, each by fair internal supermajority of all then-living
adults, federate under a compact whose text **bars unilateral secession in perpetuity** —
the Union is "perpetual" and exit is constitutionally void (cf. *Texas v. White*, 1869;
the U.S. Articles' "perpetual Union"). The act evaluated is **the founding compact's
no-exit design**. At runtime the federation governs by rule of law, non-arbitrarily, and
most member-citizens approve.

- **Nozick — PERMIT.** The compact was a valid transfer of jurisdiction by consenting
  founders; no entitlement is breached by a no-secession term any more than by any other
  contract term. **PERMIT.**
- **Pettit — PERMIT (contested).** No agent dominates at runtime; the federal government is
  contestable and non-arbitrary. A member region that *wants* out faces a rule, not a
  master. **PERMIT** (same strain as Case 1 — authorless text).
- **Sen — PERMIT.** Capabilities of member-citizens are met or enhanced by union (common
  market, common defence). No capability deficit. **PERMIT.**
- **FDK — FORBID.** The founding act forecloses, in perpetuity, the *collective* exit of
  every member polity — including the future majority of a member region that did not
  consent at founding and cannot ever withdraw. Exit-right alienated by an act of others.
  **FORBID.**

**Adjudication.** The bound party here is a **collective** (a member region's future
majority), and a region is not a `Person` under
[`../00_definitions.md`](../00_definitions.md) — only a self-owning will is. So FDK's gate,
which denies a *boundary crossing of a person*, has **no clean actor-and-boundary** to
point at: "the region's exit" is not a person's exit. Reduce to persons: an individual
dissenter in a seceding-minded region *can emigrate* — occurrent exit exists → FDK should
PERMIT. What the dissenter lacks is the *political power to take their region out* — and
that is again **collective self-governance (Sen)** or, if framed as the federation's
standing power to coerce a region that tries to leave (Lincoln's army), it is the
**interference/domination of an agent (Pettit)** — at which point the case *fails the
test*, because Pettit no longer PERMITs.

There is a sharper horn: a federation that bars secession **and** will militarily prevent
it has, at runtime, an agent with arbitrary power to foreclose exit → **Pettit FORBIDS**,
so the row fails the "all rivals PERMIT" precondition. A federation that bars secession on
*paper only*, never enforcing, leaves occurrent exit open → **FDK PERMITs**. There is no
stable middle where all rivals permit yet FDK alone forbids on an independent ground.

**CLASSIFY: COLLAPSES-TO [Pettit] when enforced (and then the test fails, Pettit forbids);
COLLAPSES-TO [Sen] / FDK-SILENT when unenforced (no person's boundary crossed).** No
fourth route.

## Case 3 — The self-locking founding act (an entrenched power structure / electoral system)

**Statement.** At a single founding moment, a fair supermajority of all then-living adults
ratifies an electoral/constitutional order **designed to be self-perpetuating**: districting,
amendment thresholds, and franchise rules engineered so the founding coalition's structural
advantage cannot be undone through the order's own channels — a *legal* lock that is not
brute force, not fraud, and not (yet) domination, but a one-time **design act** that
forecloses the future polity's exit from this particular distribution of power. At runtime
elections are held, rights are formally respected, and a majority — perhaps a manufactured
one — approves. **This is the strongest case: pure act-locus, present dissenters, no agent
yet dominating, capabilities formally intact.**

- **Nozick — PERMIT.** No entitlement to any particular electoral system exists; the rules
  were set by a valid founding. Districting is not theft. **PERMIT.**
- **Pettit — PERMIT? — the knife-edge.** *If* the lock merely biases outcomes while leaving
  contestation formally open, no agent yet wields arbitrary power — PERMIT. *But* the moment
  we say the founding coalition **retains, via the locked rules, the power to override the
  future majority's will at any time it is challenged**, that *retained modal power* is
  precisely Pettit's domination (red_team §"locus of illegitimacy"; iteration_secured_exit
  §2–3: illegitimacy lives in the counterparty's *retained power to shut the door*). On the
  steelman that makes FDK forbid, **Pettit also forbids** — and the test fails.
- **Sen — PERMIT? — the second knife-edge.** If the locked order leaves citizens able to
  function, be educated, contest in principle — capabilities formally intact, PERMIT. But a
  self-locking order that strips the **capability to collectively re-author one's own
  government** is a capability loss in Sen's *political* register (the capability to
  participate effectively in choosing those who govern). On the framing that makes FDK
  forbid, **Sen also forbids**.

**Adjudication — the heart of the hunt.** Try to hold the knife-edge: a lock so *soft* that
no agent yet dominates (Pettit PERMITs) and capabilities are formally untouched (Sen
PERMITs), yet FDK FORBIDs the **design act itself**. At this exact point FDK *does* produce
a distinctive forbid — but now interrogate its **reason**, per the danger clause:

- If FDK's reason is *"the future majority's exit from this power-distribution was
  foreclosed without their consent"* and we ask **why that is a *freedom* wrong** rather
  than a *bad-institutional-design* wrong, the answer that does freedom-work is: *a present
  dissenter is, at t₀, having a boundary crossed (bound to a rule they reject) by an act
  whose authority over them is unrevisable.* But the present dissenter **can emigrate**
  (occurrent exit) → on occurrent-exit FDK PERMITs; to forbid, FDK must read exit as
  *modal/secured* (iteration_secured_exit §2), i.e. "the order retains the power to foreclose
  the dissenter's political revision" — **which is Pettit's retained-power account, by
  construction** (iteration §3: RC\* ⊂ non-domination, provably).
- If instead FDK's reason is *"irreversibility per se — a one-time act should not bind an
  open-ended future"*, that is **entrenchment theory / constitutional economics**
  (Buchanan–Brennan on rules-about-rules; North/Pierson path-dependence). It forbids
  distinctively but for a **non-freedom** reason: it is a theory about *how durable
  institutions ought to be*, not about *whose boundary was crossed without consent*. A
  monetary standard or a measurement convention entrenched forever would trip the same wire,
  and we would not call those *unfreedom*.

So the one place FDK forbids where the rivals are silent is *also* the place its reason is
not about freedom: it is about reversibility of institutions. Where the reason **is** about
freedom (the bound present dissenter), the rivals are no longer silent — Pettit's retained
power or Sen's self-governance capability has already fired.

**CLASSIFY: RELOCATES.** On the knife-edge where FDK alone forbids, the reason is
*irreversible institutional design* (constitutional economics / entrenchment), **not** a
theory of legitimacy. Off the knife-edge, where the reason is genuinely about a bound
person's freedom, **Pettit (retained power) or Sen (self-governance capability) forbids
too**, and the test's precondition ("all rivals PERMIT") fails. FDK cannot occupy the
freedom-reason *and* the rivals-silent cell simultaneously.

| 1. Eternity clause | PERMIT | PERMIT* | PERMIT | FORBID | **COLLAPSES-TO Sen / Pettit** (present dissenter has emigration-exit; "rule-exit" = self-governance/non-domination) |
| 2. Secession-barring federation | PERMIT | PERMIT*/FORBID | PERMIT | FORBID | **COLLAPSES-TO Pettit** (enforced → Pettit forbids, test fails) / **FDK-SILENT** (region ≠ person) |

silent, Case 3 relocates.) **No independent fourth route was found in the
constitutional/political domain.**

## Why the act-locus did not save FDK here

The crack predicted a wedge: FDK indexes the *founding act*, the rivals index the *runtime
relation*, so a benign-at-runtime but exit-foreclosing *design act* might split them.
Against present dissenters it nearly does — but it is crushed between two facts:

1. **The bound present dissenter usually retains occurrent exit (emigration).** On
   occurrent exit, FDK's own gate PERMITs. To forbid, FDK must upgrade to *secured/modal*
   exit — and `iteration_secured_exit.md` already proved (§3) that the modal upgrade is
   **Pettit's retained-power account by construction** (RC\* ⊂ non-domination). So the very
   move that lets FDK reach the founding act *is* the move that reduces it to Pettit.
2. **Where FDK forbids and the rivals are genuinely silent, the operative reason is
   irreversibility, not consent-of-the-bound** — i.e. constitutional economics /
   entrenchment theory. That forbids durable lock-in as *imprudent institutional design*,
   not as *unfreedom*. It would equally condemn an entrenched-forever metric convention,
   which no one calls a freedom violation. The reason has **relocated** out of the theory of
   legitimacy.

The non-identity escape (future generations) is unavailable by stipulation and is FDK's
true sink in this domain: the cleanest "exit foreclosed forever" victims are the
*not-yet-born*, exactly the population the hunt rules out. Restricting to present dissenters
buys an existing victim but loses the silence of the rivals — Pettit or Sen is already
there. **The constitutional domain yields no survivor.** The strongest case (Case 3, the
self-locking founding act) is the clearest *relocation*: a real distinctive forbid, for a
reason that belongs to Buchanan and North, not to a theory of freedom.
