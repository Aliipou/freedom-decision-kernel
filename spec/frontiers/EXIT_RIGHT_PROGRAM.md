> The discrimination test ([`RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION.md`](RIVAL_DISCRIMINATION.md))
> and the director independently converged on the **same** conclusion: almost
> every other part of FDK either collapses back into Rothbard/Nozick or is already

>
> ```
> Freedom → Consent → Authenticity → Exit Rights → Manufactured Consent → Adaptive Preference
> ```
>
> This file is that axis as a **single falsifiable research program**, not an essay.
> It walks the phases — define, try to kill, discriminate, axiomatize, predict —
> on this one path only. Everything else in FDK is, for now, supporting structure.
>
> **The program has been executed** — see [`program/`](program/) for the worked
> phases (definitions, five enemy kill-dossiers, the discrimination table, the
> theorem ledger, the real-case/prediction file) and [`program/README.md`](program/README.md)
> for the honest convergence: one distinct-and-defensible cell (structural denial of
> lock-in / voluntary slavery), hostage to nodes **G** and **K**.

## The thesis (one sentence, stated so it can lose)

> **A transfer/arrangement is legitimate only if the consent grounding it preserves
> a real exit (revocation) right; this *structural* condition lets FDK protect the
> locked-in and the manufactured-into-consent like Sen/Rawls do — but *without*
> their welfare/interest judgment — and more determinately than Rothbard's bare
> consent.** FDK's candidate home is the empty chair: **protection without
> paternalism.**

If that chair turns out to be unoccupiable, FDK has no distinctive territory and is
"Rothbard + formal methods." That is a real, admissible outcome of this program.

## Phase 1 — Definitions sharp enough that two readers cannot diverge

The program is not ready until each term below has a definition on which two honest
readers reach the **same verdict** on a contested case.

- **Consent** — an attestation by a rights-holder authorising a boundary crossing.
  *Not* preference, *not* satisfaction, *not* the absence of objection.
- **Exit / revocation right** — the standing, exercisable ability to withdraw from
  the arrangement and undo future obligation, at a cost that does not itself
  destroy the person (ruin, death, statelessness). The hard edge: **how much cost
  is "exit-defeating"?** Undefined cost makes the whole thesis vacuous — this is
  the first thing to pin (see open problems).
- **Authentic consent** — consent whose exit right is real *and* whose formation
  was not engineered to foreclose its own withdrawal (addiction, debt traps,
  designed-for-capture choice architecture).
- **Manufactured consent** — sincere consent whose *conditions of formation* were
  arranged by an interested party to remove real alternatives. There is a will, and
  it is genuine; the wrong is upstream, in how the will was produced.
- **Adaptive preference** (the killer) — sincere consent where the person *no longer
  wants the exit*, the preference having adapted to the deprivation. No manipulator
  need exist. (Sen.)

**Concept separations this axis depends on** (collapse any one and the program
explodes): `Consent ≠ Preference`, `Exit-right ≠ Exit actually taken`,
`Authenticity ≠ Sincerity`, `Legitimacy ≠ Legality`, `Freedom ≠ Welfare`.

## Phase 2 — The kill condition (write the falsifier first)

A thesis with no falsifier is not science. FDK's exit-right thesis is false if:

> **If a person's consent is sincere, the arrangement leaves a real exit available,
> and they nonetheless do *not* want to take it because their preference has adapted
> to subordination (the contented slave) — and our considered judgment is that the
> arrangement is illegitimate — then a purely structural exit test UNDER-protects,
> and FDK must either (a) import an interest-judgment (becoming Sen, losing
> "without paternalism") or (b) accept the verdict (becoming Rothbard).**

That is the single experiment that decides the program. Adaptive preference is not
one objection among many; it is *the* wall. If exit-right survives it, FDK breathes.
If not, FDK has no distinctive, defensible ground and that should be stated plainly.

A second, milder falsifier: **manufactured consent with a nominal exit** (dark
patterns, C3) — if exit *exists on paper* but the arrangement is still illegitimate,
the exit must be defined richly enough (Phase 1's cost edge) to catch it without
collapsing into "any bad option = no exit."

## Phase 3 — Discrimination on the axis cases (the only table that matters)

Re-run the rivals **only on the consent axis**, at the margins, not the clean cases.

| Case | Rothbard | Rawls | Sen | **FDK (exit-right)** | Distinct & right? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Child binds self to lifetime labor | indeterminate | protect | protect (capability) | **DENY** — no valid authority-to-transfer; exit foreclosed | candidate ✚ (more determinate than Rothbard, no welfare) |
| Company town / platform lock-in | ALLOW | ~DENY | DENY | **DENY** — exit right removed | candidate ✚ |
| Voluntary lifetime slavery contract | ALLOW (some) | DENY | DENY | **DENY** — alienates the exit right itself | candidate ✚ |
| Manufactured consent, exit on paper | ALLOW | mixed | DENY | **? hinges on the cost edge (Phase 1)** | undecided |
| Adaptive preference / contented slave | ALLOW | mixed | **DENY** | **ALLOW** (exit unwanted) | **✗ — FDK loses to Sen here** |
| Monopoly "take it or leave it" | ALLOW | mixed | DENY | **? exit exists but ruinous** | undecided — depends on cost edge |

The shape to notice: FDK's candidate wins (rows 1–3) and its decisive loss (row 5)
are the *same mechanism* seen from two sides. Exit-right protects where the victim
*would* leave if they could; it fails where the victim no longer *wants* to. The
whole program lives or dies on whether that line is defensible.

## Phase 4 — From prose to theorems (stop writing essays)

The axis, axiomatized. Each line is meant to be proved or broken, not admired.

```text
A-C   (axiom)     A boundary crossing is legitimate only with the valid consent of
                  the boundary's owner.
T1    (theorem)   Every legitimate transfer requires valid consent.            [A-C]
T2    (theorem)   Consent is valid only if revocable: a consent that cannot be
                  withdrawn alienates the exit right, which is not the owner's to
                  alienate (it is constitutive of ownership).                   [A-C]
T3    (corollary) ⇒ No valid contract can foreclose its own exit (voluntary
                  lifetime slavery is illegitimate even if sincere).         [T1,T2]
T4    (corollary) ⇒ An arrangement that structurally removes all real exit is
                  illegitimate regardless of stated satisfaction (lock-in).  [T2]
G     (open gap)  T2's "revocable" requires the cost-of-exit threshold of Phase 1.
                  Until G is closed, T3/T4 are schematic, not theorems.
K     (open kill) Adaptive preference: the owner sincerely affirms the
                  non-revocable arrangement. Does T2 deny their *sincere* consent
                  without importing an interest-judgment? If no → FDK ⊂ Sen here.
```

The chain `A-C → T1 → T2 → T3/T4 → Decision` is the inference spine. Note honestly
that **G and K are unproven** — the program's value is concentrated in exactly
those two open nodes, not in the parts that already follow.

## Phase 5 — Real cases and a prediction (the part that makes it science)

Explaining the past is not enough; the thesis must stick its neck out.

- **Retrodiction (necessary, not sufficient):** historical slavery, colonial labor,
  company towns, debt bondage, cult membership, platform lock-in — does the
  exit-right reading sort the legitimate from the illegitimate *better than* both
  bare-consent and welfare readings? (This is `RISKY_PREDICTIONS.md` P1.)
- **Prediction (the real test):**
  > Across regimes matched on stated satisfaction, those whose consent is
  > exit-poor (company towns, debt bondage, high-lock-in platforms) exhibit
  > measurably **lower realised mobility and higher coerced-stay** than exit-rich
  > regimes — *and this gap, not the satisfaction level, tracks the
  > legitimate/illegitimate judgment.*
  > **Refuted if** exit-poor and exit-rich regimes are behaviorally
  > indistinguishable at equal satisfaction — then "exit" has no signature and FDK
  > is chasing a phantom.

## The win condition (what "finishing" actually requires)

FDK starts to breathe the moment it can show **one** real problem where its
exit-right reading is simultaneously **(a) different** from Rothbard/Nozick,
**(b) internally consistent**, and **(c) better** (more determinate, or protective
without paternalism) than Rawls/Sen. The strongest current candidate is **voluntary
lifetime slavery / lock-in** (rows 1–3): FDK denies it *structurally* (T2/T3), where
Rothbard cannot and Sen can only by overriding the will. If that result survives the
adaptive-preference kill (K), FDK has its first genuine contribution. If it does not,
the honest report is that FDK is a clean engineering restatement of libertarian
consent — and that, too, is a finding worth publishing.

## Open problems (where the 90% of remaining value sits — and it is not code)

1. **The exit-cost threshold (G).** When is exit so costly it is no exit? Any answer
   risks smuggling welfare back in. This is the technical heart.
2. **Adaptive preference (K).** Can a structural test deny sincere consent without
   paternalism? Possibly not — and that would bound FDK's distinctive region to
   "manufactured but not yet internalised" consent.
3. **Ownership genesis** stays the deepest *shared* hole (why does first use bind?),
   but it is not on this axis and not where FDK is distinctive — see
   [[ownership_genesis]]. Pursue the exit axis first; it is the only ✚.
