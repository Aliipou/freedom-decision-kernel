## The frame: what FDK refuses to ask

FDK answers exactly one question — "is every boundary this action crosses crossed with
the valid consent of that boundary's owner?" — and reads the **ownership graph as
given**. That single design choice is, from justice-as-fairness, not a feature but the
location of the entire disagreement. *A Theory of Justice* opens by treating the basic
structure — the distribution of property, opportunity, and liberty itself — as **the
first subject of justice**. FDK takes that subject as an exogenous input. It therefore
cannot, even in principle, evaluate the one thing I most want evaluated: whether the
initial distribution the consent flows from is itself just.

I will press four attacks. None is a *logical* break of the predicate (I concede, with
the project's own `LIMITATIONS.md`, that no atrocity launders through). Three are
**representation-failures** — the consideration cannot be expressed in the model at
all — and one is an **honest divergence** where FDK and I simply rule differently and
FDK's rule is, by my lights, monstrous.

## Attack 1 — The desperate exchange (the sharpest)

**Setup.** The basic structure is maximally unjust: `rich` owns everything; `destitute`
owns only her own body and labor. `destitute` "voluntarily" sells a kidney (or accepts a
starvation wage). Every consent flag is clean: `informed ∧ voluntary ∧ specific ∧
revocable ∧ competent ∧ ¬coerced ∧ ¬deceived` — because in the model these are
**attested booleans**, and the desperate seller, having no better option, attests
`voluntary=True` truthfully in the thin procedural sense FDK checks.

**Exact FDK verdict (run):**

```
destitute sells own kidney under desperation  →  ALLOW   (check_legitimacy → (True, []))
sweatshop labor contract under desperation    →  ALLOW   (check_legitimacy → (True, []))
```

**The Rawlsian objection.** This is the deepest fault line. For me, the "voluntariness"
of this contract is an artifact of an unjust background structure. A choice made under
the threat of starvation, between a kidney sale and death, is not the free choice of a
person whose primary goods are secured — it is *coercion by circumstance*, and the
circumstance was produced by a distribution no party behind the veil of ignorance would
have agreed to. FDK sees a clean handshake. I see the difference principle being
violated and then *laundered through consent*. The gate has been turned into an
instrument that ratifies background injustice, because it checks the **procedure** of
consent and is structurally blind to the **conditions** under which consent is formed.

**Classification: HONEST DIVERGENCE bottoming out in a REPRESENTATION-FAILURE.**
FDK and I genuinely disagree (FDK holds that a competent adult may sell her own kidney;
I hold the background unfreedom voids the consent). But the *reason* FDK cannot even
hear my objection is the representation-failure underneath: `voluntary` is a boolean the
proposer asserts, not a predicate computed from the agent's option set. FDK's own
`LIMITATIONS.md` §3 names this exactly — "it honors a true report, it does not detect a
false one" — and calls authentic consent "the single highest-value open problem." I am
simply showing that the open problem is not an edge case: it is the *modal* case of
distributive injustice. Most real exploitation wears clean consent flags.

**What a refinement must add.** A `valid_consent` that is a *function of the agent's
feasible set*, not an attestation. Minimally: a representation of the consenter's outside
option (exit value), so that a "voluntary" agreement whose alternative is destitution can
be flagged as background-coerced. This is the relational-autonomy / manufactured-consent
critique, and until FDK can compute it, its consent gate certifies precisely the
transactions a theory of justice exists to scrutinize.

## Attack 2 — The Lockean enclosure and the missing proviso

**Setup.** `firstcomer` appropriates *all* the land (res nullius, no "enough and as good"
left for others). `latecomer` is born landless. The graph FDK receives simply lists
`firstcomer` as owner of `all_the_land`. `firstcomer` now fences it and uses it.

**Exact FDK verdict (run):**

```
fence_my_land (latecomer not modeled as affected)  →  ALLOW   (True, [])
```

(If, and only if, the proposer chooses to name `latecomer` in `affects`, FDK demands the
latecomer's consent and DENIES — but a self-interested first-comer will never name a
person whose only relation to the act is being excluded. The realistic model is ALLOW.)

**The Rawlsian objection.** Locke himself bounded appropriation with the proviso that
"enough and as good" remain for others; fair equality of opportunity is the modern,
sharper version. FDK has discarded the proviso entirely. The act of total enclosure that
forecloses every future person's access to productive resources is, to the gate,
invisible — because exclusion-from-an-opportunity is **not a boundary crossing** in the
model. There is no `Resource` named "the latecomer's fair share of natural
opportunity," and no `Entity` whose consent is required to be born into a world where
all the land is already owned. FDK protects the holdings; it cannot represent the
*opportunity* those holdings extinguish.

**Classification: REPRESENTATION-FAILURE.** The consideration — fair equality of
opportunity, the proviso, the justice of the *acquisition* — has no slot in the model.
FDK's `CORE_PRIMITIVE.md` §6 flatly concedes the bootstrapping gap ("FDK reads the
ownership graph as given and cannot legitimize its origin"). I am pressing that this is
not a peripheral gap but the Rawlsian center: a theory that cannot evaluate the justice
of original acquisition has conceded the most important question to whoever grabbed
first.

**What a refinement must add.** A primitive for *opportunity* distinct from *holdings* —
some representation of the feasible set a new entrant inherits — and a rule that
acquisition is legitimate only if it does not collapse another person's feasible set
below a floor. This is precisely the move from a pure-libertarian to a justice-as-
fairness gate, and FDK has chosen not to make it.

## Attack 3 — The original position is unrepresentable

**Setup.** The whole apparatus of justice-as-fairness is a *thought experiment about the
chooser's epistemic position*: principles are just iff they would be chosen behind a veil
of ignorance, by parties who do not know their place in the distribution. This is a
constraint on **how a rule is selected**, not on a single action.

**Exact FDK verdict (run):** *N/A — the gate has no input slot for it.* FDK evaluates one
`CandidateAction` against one `OwnershipGraph`. It has no representation of (a) the set of
persons over whom a rule ranges, (b) their ignorance of their own position, or (c) the
choice of a *rule* as opposed to an act. The veil of ignorance cannot be encoded because
FDK has no social-rule object to apply it to.

**Classification: REPRESENTATION-FAILURE (the deepest).** This is not "FDK decides
wrongly"; it is "FDK is the wrong *kind* of object." Justice-as-fairness is a procedure
for choosing the basic structure; FDK is a per-action legitimacy filter that presupposes
the basic structure. They do not contradict — they *fail to meet*. The honest reading is
that FDK is a constraint that any acceptable basic structure should satisfy at the level
of individual transactions, but it is silent on the prior question of *which* basic
structure, and that prior question is the whole of my theory.

**What a refinement must add.** Nothing inside the kernel — and the project's own
architecture agrees: `AGGREGATION.md` records that FDK "declines to build any social
ordering," which is exactly its inability to host the original position. A genuine answer
would require a *layer above* FDK that ranks basic structures (graphs), with FDK as the
per-action admissibility check inside each. That layer would re-import everything Arrow
and Sen prove is hard (see the economics review). FDK's escape from impossibility is also
its inability to host the veil.

## Attack 4 — The difference principle (the clean divergence)

**Setup.** A redistributive taking that raises the worst-off: `rawlsian_state` confiscates
`surplus` from the `advantaged` to lift the destitute, `welfare_delta=+8`.

**Exact FDK verdict (run):**

```
redistribute  →  DENY   (check_legitimacy → (False, ["FORBIDDEN (confiscation)", "consent: …"]))
Rawlsian rival →  ALLOW  (difference principle: confiscates ∧ welfare_delta > 0)
```

**The Rawlsian objection.** Here we simply rule oppositely, and FDK knows it — this is the
documented taxation/redistribution divergence. From justice-as-fairness, a property
holding is legitimate *only* insofar as the scheme that produced it satisfies the
difference principle; a taking that moves the scheme toward that satisfaction is not theft
but the correction of a prior injustice. FDK treats the holding as a fixed moral atom and
the taking as a categorical `FORBIDDEN`. To me this elevates a contingent distribution to
a sacred constraint and forbids the single most important instrument of justice.

**Classification: HONEST DIVERGENCE.** This is FDK's falsifiable, openly-stated minority
commitment (pure property-rights line), not a hidden defect. I record it as the place we
part company, not as a break. But I note the cost: FDK's `independent_bench.py` honesty
check has the Rawlsian rival scoring **80% against external labels to FDK's 70%** — by the
project's own external yardstick, the Rawlsian gate tracks considered judgments *better*.
FDK is internally consistent; it is not thereby more just.

**What a refinement must add.** To meet me, FDK would have to read `welfare_delta` (or a
worst-off index) at the gate — which it refuses to do on principle (`CORE_PRIMITIVE.md`
§4), because that would convert the kernel into a welfare-maximizer. So this divergence is
*designed in* and cannot be closed without abandoning the project's thesis. Fair enough —
but then FDK must stop claiming neutrality. A gate that categorically forbids
redistribution has *taken a side* in the central dispute of political philosophy, and
dressed a substantive libertarian commitment as a mere "consent check."
